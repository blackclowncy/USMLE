## 1. Cardiovascular Disorders

### 1.1 Acute Coronary Syndrome & Acute Myocardial Infarction
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACC/AHA (American College of Cardiology / American Heart Association) Guidelines, ESC Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Decision Rules**:
  - Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI) requires detection of a rise and/or fall of cardiac biomarkers (preferably Cardiac Troponin cTnI or cTnT above the 99th percentile upper reference limit) with at least one evidence of myocardial ischemia:
    1. Ischemic clinical symptoms (crushing substernal chest pressure radiating to left shoulder, arm, or jaw);
    2. New ischemic ECG changes (new ST-segment deviation or new Left Bundle Branch Block [LBBB]);
    3. Development of pathological Q waves on ECG;
    4. Imaging evidence of new loss of viable myocardium or new regional wall motion abnormality;
    5. Identification of an intracoronary thrombus by angiography.
- **Core Laboratory Biomarkers & Kinetics**:
  - **Cardiac Troponin I (cTnI) / Troponin T (cTnT)**:
    - Onset: 3–6 hours post-infarction;
    - Peak: 14–24 hours;
    - Duration: cTnI remains elevated for 7–10 days; cTnT remains elevated for 10–14 days.
    - **Clinical Significance**: Gold standard biomarker with the highest sensitivity and cardiac specificity.
  - **CK-MB (Creatine Kinase-MB Isoenzyme)**:
    - Onset: 3–6 hours; Peak: 12–24 hours; Normalization: 48–72 hours.
    - **CK-MB Relative Index**: CK-MB (ng/mL) / Total CK (U/L) × 100. Ratio > 2.5%–3.0% indicates myocardial necrosis; gold standard for diagnosing **early reinfarction**.
  - **Myoglobin**:
    - Onset: 1–2 hours (earliest marker); high negative predictive value; lacks cardiac specificity.
- **Manual Test References**: `Troponin` (P.587), `Creatine Kinase` (P.201), `Creatine Kinase-MB` (P.203), `Myoglobin` (P.418).

### 1.2 Congestive Heart Failure & Decompensated HF
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: HFSA (Heart Failure Society of America, 2006), ACC/AHA Heart Failure Guidelines.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Decision Rules**:
  - Acute decompensated heart failure is diagnosed based on clinical symptoms (dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, elevated jugular venous pressure, bilateral lower extremity edema) combined with quantitative natriuretic peptide measurement (BNP or NT-proBNP).
- **Core Laboratory Diagnostic Cutoffs**:
  - **B-Type Natriuretic Peptide (BNP)**:
    - **< 100 pg/mL**: Heart failure excluded (negative predictive value > 90%);
    - **100–400 pg/mL**: Gray zone (requires correlation with renal function, age, atrial fibrillation);
    - **> 400 pg/mL**: Strong evidence supporting acute heart failure.
  - **NT-proBNP (Age-Stratified Cutoffs)**:
    - Rule-out threshold: < 300 pg/mL (all ages);
    - Rule-in thresholds:
      - Age < 50 years: > 450 pg/mL;
      - Age 50–75 years: > 900 pg/mL;
      - Age > 75 years: > 1800 pg/mL;
    - Renal insufficiency (eGFR < 60 mL/min): Cutoff adjusted to > 1200 pg/mL.
- **Echocardiography Classification**:
  - HFrEF (Reduced Ejection Fraction): LVEF < 40%;
  - HFpEF (Preserved Ejection Fraction): LVEF ≥ 50% with evidence of diastolic dysfunction and elevated filling pressures.
- **Manual Test References**: `B-Type Natriuretic Peptide` (P.420), `Echocardiography` (P.232).

### 1.3 Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: USPSTF (U.S. Preventive Services Task Force) Screening Guidelines.
- **Screening & Diagnostic Criteria**:
  - **USPSTF Screening Recommendation**: One-time abdominal ultrasound screening is strongly recommended for **men aged 65 to 75 who have ever smoked (≥100 cigarettes)**.
  - In men aged 65–75 who have never smoked, selective screening is based on individual risk; routine screening in women is not recommended.
- **Core Ultrasonographic Diagnostic Cutoffs**:
  - **Normal Aortic Caliber**: Normal adult abdominal aorta is < 2.0–2.5 cm.
  - **Aneurysm Definition**: Focal dilatation with aortic diameter **≥ 3.0 cm** (or > 50% increase over adjacent normal caliber).
  - **Clinical Intervention & Rupture Risk Thresholds**:
    - Diameter 3.0–3.9 cm: Annual ultrasound surveillance;
    - Diameter 4.0–5.4 cm: Ultrasound surveillance every 6 months;
    - **Diameter ≥ 5.5 cm in men or ≥ 5.0 cm in women**, or expansion rate **> 0.5 cm per 6 months**: Surgical repair indication (EVAR or open surgical graft).
- **Manual Test References**: `Abdominal Aorta Sonogram` (P.18).

### 1.4 Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Wells DVT Clinical Probability Score, ACCP Guidelines.
- **Stepwise Diagnostic Algorithm**:
  1. **Clinical Pretest Probability (Wells Score for DVT)**:
     - Predictors (+1 each): Active cancer, paralysis/plaster immobilization, bedridden >3 days or major surgery within 12 weeks, localized tenderness along deep venous system, entire leg swollen, calf circumference >3 cm greater than asymptomatic side, pitting edema greater in symptomatic leg, collateral superficial veins, previous documented DVT; Alternative diagnosis at least as likely as DVT (-2 points).
     - **Low Probability**: Score ≤ 0;
     - **Moderate Probability**: Score 1–2;
     - **High Probability**: Score ≥ 3.
  2. **D-Dimer Testing**:
     - Cutoff: < 500 ng/mL FEU.
     - **Clinical Rule**: In low or moderate probability patients, a negative high-sensitivity D-dimer safely rules out DVT without imaging; in high-probability patients, ultrasound is mandatory regardless of D-dimer.
  3. **Compression Ultrasonography (CUS)**:
     - **Gold Standard Diagnostic Sign**: **Lack of compressibility** of the deep venous lumen under transducer pressure, with intraluminal echoes and Doppler flow filling defects.
- **Manual Test References**: `D-Dimer Test` (P.228), `Venous Doppler Ultrasound` (P.228).

### 1.5 Pulmonary Embolism (PE)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACEP (American College of Emergency Physicians) Clinical Practice Guidelines, Wells PE Criteria.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Decision Pathway**:
  - **Wells PE Score Stratification**:
    - HR > 100 bpm (+1.5), surgery/immobilization within 4 weeks (+1.5), previous DVT/PE (+1.5), hemoptysis (+1), active malignancy (+1), clinical signs of DVT (+3), PE most likely diagnosis (+3).
    - Low probability: 0–1; Moderate probability: 2–6; High probability: ≥ 7.
  - **D-Dimer Testing**: High-sensitivity D-dimer (< 500 ng/mL) rules out PE in low/intermediate probability groups.
  - **CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA)**: Definitive gold standard imaging. Diagnostic finding: intraluminal filling defect (saddle embolus, total or partial vascular occlusion).
  - **Ventilation/Perfusion (V/Q) Scan**: Indicated in severe renal insufficiency or contrast allergy. High probability criteria: ≥ 2 large segmental mismatched perfusion defects with normal ventilation.
- **Manual Test References**: `D-Dimer Test` (P.228), `CTPA` (P.388), `Lung Ventilation and Perfusion Scan` (P.388).

### 1.6 Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACC/AHA Practice Guidelines for the Management of Patients with Peripheral Arterial Disease.
- **Ankle-Brachial Index (ABI) Diagnostic Classification**:
  - **Measurement**: Ratio of highest systolic blood pressure in the dorsalis pedis or posterior tibial artery divided by the highest brachial artery systolic pressure.
  - **Diagnostic Tiers**:
    - **1.00 – 1.40**: Normal lower extremity perfusion;
    - **0.91 – 0.99**: Borderline perfusion;
    - **0.71 – 0.90**: Mild peripheral arterial disease;
    - **0.41 – 0.70**: Moderate PAD (typical intermittent claudication);
    - **≤ 0.40**: Severe PAD / Critical limb ischemia (rest pain, nonhealing ulcers, tissue gangrene);
    - **> 1.40**: Incompressible, severely calcified arteries (Mönckeberg sclerosis; common in long-standing diabetes or ESRD; requires Toe-Brachial Index [TBI]).
- **Manual Test References**: `Ankle-Brachial Index` (P.57).

### 1.7 Infective Endocarditis (IE)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Modified Duke Diagnostic Criteria.
- **Definitive Diagnosis Requirement**: 2 Major Criteria, OR 1 Major + 3 Minor Criteria, OR 5 Minor Criteria.
- **Major Criteria**:
  1. **Positive Blood Cultures**: Typical microorganisms (Viridans streptococci, S. bovis, HACEK, S. aureus, enterococci) from 2 separate blood cultures, or persistently positive cultures draw >12 hours apart.
  2. **Evidence of Endocardial Involvement**: Echocardiogram demonstrating oscillating intracardiac mass/vegetation on valve or supporting structures, abscess, new partial dehiscence of prosthetic valve, or new valvular regurgitation.
- **Minor Criteria**:
  1. Predisposition (heart condition or IV drug use);
  2. Fever ≥ 38.0°C (100.4°F);
  3. Vascular phenomena (arterial emboli, septic pulmonary infarcts, mycotic aneurysm, intracranial hemorrhage, Janeway lesions);
  4. Immunologic phenomena (glomerulonephritis, Osler nodes, Roth spots, Rheumatoid Factor);
  5. Microbiologic evidence (positive blood culture not meeting major criterion).
- **Manual Test References**: `Blood Culture and Sensitivity` (P.114), `Echocardiography` (P.232).

### 1.8 Dyslipidemia & Cardiovascular Risk (NCEP ATP III)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: National Cholesterol Education Program (NCEP) Adult Treatment Panel III (ATP III).
- **Fasting Lipid Profile Classification (mg/dL)**:
  - **LDL Cholesterol (Primary Target)**:
    - < 100: Optimal;
    - 100–129: Near optimal;
    - 130–159: Borderline high;
    - 160–189: High;
    - ≥ 190: Very high (requires intensive statin therapy).
  - **Total Cholesterol**:
    - < 200: Desirable;
    - 200–239: Borderline high;
    - ≥ 240: High.
  - **HDL Cholesterol**:
    - < 40 in men, < 50 in women: Major independent cardiovascular risk factor;
    - ≥ 60: Protective ("negative risk factor").
  - **Serum Triglycerides**:
    - < 150: Normal;
    - 150–199: Borderline high;
    - 200–499: High;
    - ≥ 500: Very high (immediate risk of acute pancreatitis).
- **Manual Test References**: `Cholesterol` (P.189), `Triglycerides` (P.582), `Lipoprotein Electrophoresis` (P.383).

### 1.9 Metabolic Syndrome (ATP III Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: NCEP ATP III Clinical Criteria.
- **Diagnostic Requirement**: Presence of **≥ 3 of the following 5 criteria**:
  1. **Abdominal Obesity**: Waist circumference > 102 cm (> 40 in) in men, > 88 cm (> 35 in) in women;
  2. **Hypertriglyceridemia**: Triglycerides ≥ 150 mg/dL (1.7 mmol/L), or on drug treatment;
  3. **Low HDL Cholesterol**: HDL-C < 40 mg/dL (1.0 mmol/L) in men, < 50 mg/dL (1.3 mmol/L) in women;
  4. **Elevated Blood Pressure**: Systolic BP ≥ 130 mmHg and/or Diastolic BP ≥ 85 mmHg, or on antihypertensive therapy;
  5. **Impaired Fasting Glucose**: Fasting plasma glucose ≥ 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L), or on drug therapy for hyperglycemia.
- **Manual Test References**: `Glucose, Blood` (P.280), `Triglycerides` (P.582), `Cholesterol` (P.189).

## 2. Endocrine & Metabolic Disorders

### 2.1 Diabetes Mellitus (ADA Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: American Diabetes Association (ADA) Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes.
- **Diagnostic Criteria** (Requires repeat testing on a separate day unless classic hyperglycemia crisis is present):
  1. **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG)**: ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) after fasting for ≥ 8 hours;
  2. **2-Hour Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT)**: ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) following a 75-g anhydrous glucose load;
  3. **Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c)**: ≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) standardized to NGSP assay;
  4. **Random Plasma Glucose**: ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) in a patient with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss) or hyperglycemic crisis.
- **Manual Test References**: `Glucose, Blood` (P.280), `Glucose Tolerance Test` (P.283), `Glycohemoglobin` (P.286).

---

### 2.2 Prediabetes: IFG & IGT
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ADA Clinical Practice Recommendations.
- **Classification & Cutoffs**:
  - **Impaired Fasting Glucose (IFG)**: Fasting Plasma Glucose 100–125 mg/dL (5.6–6.9 mmol/L);
  - **Impaired Glucose Tolerance (IGT)**: 2-hour plasma glucose in 75-g OGTT 140–199 mg/dL (7.8–11.0 mmol/L);
  - **HbA1c Prediabetes Range**: 5.7% – 6.4% (39–47 mmol/mol). High risk of developing frank diabetes and cardiovascular disease.
- **Manual Test References**: `Glucose, Blood` (P.280), `Glycohemoglobin` (P.286).

---

### 2.3 Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACOG & ADA Two-Step Strategy (O'Sullivan Criteria).
- **Two-Step Screening & Diagnostic Strategy**:
  1. **Step 1 (50g Glucose Challenge Test [GCT])**: Performed at 24–28 weeks of gestation without regard to time of day or meal. If 1-hour plasma glucose is ≥ 130–140 mg/dL (7.2–7.8 mmol/L), proceed to Step 2.
  2. **Step 2 (100g 3-Hour Diagnostic OGTT)**: Diagnosis established if **≥ 2 values** meet or exceed the following:
     - Fasting: ≥ 95 mg/dL (5.3 mmol/L);
     - 1-hour: ≥ 180 mg/dL (10.0 mmol/L);
     - 2-hour: ≥ 155 mg/dL (8.6 mmol/L);
     - 3-hour: ≥ 140 mg/dL (7.8 mmol/L).
- **Manual Test References**: `Glucose Tolerance Test` (P.283).

---

### 2.4 Diabetic Nephropathy & Microalbuminuria Screening
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ADA & KDOQI Clinical Guidelines.
- **Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (UACR) Diagnostic Criteria**:
  - **Normal**: UACR < 30 mcg/mg creatinine (< 3 mg/mmol);
  - **Microalbuminuria (Persistent Early Renal Damage)**: UACR **30–299 mcg/mg** creatinine on at least 2 of 3 spot urine collections over a 3- to 6-month period;
  - **Macroalbuminuria (Overt Diabetic Nephropathy)**: UACR **≥ 300 mcg/mg** creatinine.
  - **Screening Schedule**: Annual screening starting 5 years post-diagnosis in Type 1 DM, and immediately at diagnosis in Type 2 DM.
- **Manual Test References**: `Microalbuminuria` (P.415), `Creatinine, Urine` (P.206).

---

### 2.5 Cushing Syndrome & Hypercortisolism
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guidelines.
- **Stepwise Diagnostic Algorithm**:
  1. **Step 1: Confirm Hypercortisolism** (requires at least 2 abnormal first-line tests):
     - 24-hour urinary free cortisol (UFC) > 3–4 times upper limit of normal;
     - Late-night salivary cortisol > 145 ng/dL (4 nmol/L);
     - Overnight 1-mg low-dose dexamethasone suppression test (DST): Morning 8:00 AM serum cortisol fails to suppress (< 1.8 mcg/dL or < 50 nmol/L).
  2. **Step 2: Plasma ACTH Measurement**:
     - ACTH < 5 pg/mL: ACTH-independent (Adrenal adenoma/carcinoma; obtain adrenal CT);
     - ACTH > 15–20 pg/mL: ACTH-dependent (Cushing disease vs. Ectopic ACTH secretion).
  3. **Step 3: High-Dose Dexamethasone Suppression (8-mg DST)**:
     - Cortisol suppresses by > 50%: Pituitary Cushing Disease;
     - Fails to suppress: Ectopic ACTH production (e.g., Small Cell Lung Cancer).
- **Manual Test References**: `Cortisol` (P.198), `Adrenocorticotropic Hormone` (P.26), `Dexamethasone Suppression Test` (P.218).

---

### 2.6 Adrenal Insufficiency & Addison Disease
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Endocrine Society Clinical Guidelines.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Decision Rules**:
  - **Morning Basal Cortisol (8:00 AM)**:
    - < 3 mcg/dL (< 80 nmol/L): Strongly suggestive of adrenal insufficiency;
    - > 18 mcg/dL (> 500 nmol/L): Adrenal insufficiency excluded.
  - **High-Dose Cosyntropin (ACTH) Stimulation Test (Gold Standard)**:
    - Administration of 250 mcg IV/IM synthetic ACTH (Cosyntropin); measure serum cortisol at 0, 30, and 60 minutes.
    - **Normal**: Peak serum cortisol **≥ 18–20 mcg/dL** (≥ 500 nmol/L).
    - **Primary Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison)**: Peak cortisol fails to reach 18 mcg/dL, accompanied by high plasma ACTH (> 100 pg/mL) and elevated plasma renin with hyperkalemia/hyponatremia.
    - **Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency**: Pituitary failure with low basal ACTH; blunted response on prolonged stimulation.
- **Manual Test References**: `Adrenocorticotropic Hormone Stimulation Test` (P.28), `Cortisol` (P.198).

---

### 2.7 Primary Hyperaldosteronism (Conn Syndrome)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Endocrine Society Practice Guidelines.
- **Screening & Diagnostic Criteria**:
  - **Screening Test**: Morning serum Aldosterone-to-Plasma Renin Activity Ratio (**ARR**).
  - **Positive Screening Threshold**: ARR **≥ 20–30** with concurrent serum aldosterone **≥ 15 ng/dL** (416 pmol/L) and suppressed plasma renin activity (PRA < 1.0 ng/mL/h).
  - **Confirmatory Testing**: Oral sodium loading test or IV saline infusion (2 L over 4 hours): post-infusion plasma aldosterone > 10 ng/dL confirms autonomous primary hyperaldosteronism.
- **Manual Test References**: `Aldosterone` (P.32), `Renin` (P.512).

---

### 2.8 Pheochromocytoma
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Endocrine Society Guidelines.
- **Biochemical Diagnostic Criteria**:
  - **High-risk patients**: Plasma free metanephrines (sensitivity 96–100%); levels > 3–4 times upper limit of normal confirm catecholamine-secreting tumor.
  - **Standard-risk patients**: 24-hour urine fractionated metanephrines and catecholamines (high specificity).
  - **Rule-out Rule**: Normal plasma free metanephrines and urine metanephrines reliably excludes pheochromocytoma.
- **Manual Test References**: `Catecholamines` (P.158), `Metanephrines, Urine` (P.410), `Vanillylmandelic Acid` (P.601).

---

### 2.9 Acromegaly & Gigantism
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Endocrine Society Practice Guidelines.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Laboratory Pathway**:
  1. **Initial Screening**: Serum Insulin-Like Growth Factor 1 (IGF-1 / Somatomedin C). Elevated age- and sex-adjusted IGF-1 has >95% sensitivity.
  2. **Confirmatory Gold Standard**: Oral Glucose Tolerance Test for GH Suppression (OGTT-GH Suppression Test). Normal response: 75g oral glucose suppresses serum GH to **< 1.0 ng/mL** (ultra-sensitive assay < 0.4 ng/mL). Failure of suppression confirms acromegaly.
- **Manual Test References**: `Growth Hormone` (P.293), `Somatomedin C` (P.554).

---

### 2.10 Hyperthyroidism & Graves Disease
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ATA / AACE Practice Guidelines for Hyperthyroidism.
- **Biochemical Diagnostic Patterns**:
  - **Overt Primary Hyperthyroidism**: Serum TSH **< 0.01 mU/L** (suppressed) with elevated Free T4 and/or Free T3.
  - **Subclinical Hyperthyroidism**: Suppressed TSH with normal Free T4 and Free T3.
  - **Graves Disease Differentiation**: Diffuse elevated radioactive iodine uptake (RAIU) and positive **TSH Receptor Antibodies (TRAb / TSI)**.
- **Manual Test References**: `Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone` (P.569), `Thyroxine, Free` (P.573), `Thyroid Scan` (P.571).

---

### 2.11 Hypothyroidism & Subclinical Hypothyroidism
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ATA / AACE Practice Guidelines for Hypothyroidism.
- **Diagnostic Classification**:
  - **Primary Overt Hypothyroidism**: Elevated TSH (> 10 mU/L) with decreased Free T4.
  - **Subclinical Hypothyroidism**: Elevated TSH (4.5–10 mU/L) with normal Free T4.
  - **Hashimoto Thyroiditis Confirmation**: Positive anti-thyroid peroxidase (anti-TPO) and anti-thyroglobulin (anti-Tg) antibodies.
  - **Central Hypothyroidism**: Low or inappropriately normal TSH with low Free T4 (pituitary/hypothalamic pathology).
- **Manual Test References**: `Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone` (P.569), `Thyroxine, Free` (P.573), `Antimicrosomal Antibody Test` (P.67).

---

### 2.12 Primary Hyperparathyroidism
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: International Workshop on the Management of Asymptomatic Primary Hyperparathyroidism.
- **Biochemical Diagnostic Triad**:
  1. **Hypercalcemia**: Serum total calcium > 10.5 mg/dL (or elevated ionized calcium);
  2. **Inappropriately Elevated or Normal Intact PTH**: Serum intact PTH elevated or within upper-normal range despite hypercalcemia;
  3. **Hypophosphatemia**: Decreased serum phosphate (< 2.5 mg/dL) with elevated urinary calcium excretion.
  - **Differentiation from Familial Hypocalciuric Hypercalcemia (FHH)**: 24-hour urine calcium clearance to creatinine clearance ratio (CCCR) < 0.01 indicates FHH; CCCR > 0.02 indicates primary hyperparathyroidism.
- **Manual Test References**: `Calcium, Blood` (P.142), `Parathyroid Hormone` (P.458), `Phosphorus` (P.470).

---

### 2.13 Male Hypogonadism / Androgen Deficiency
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Endocrine Society Guidelines.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Workup**:
  - Requires symptoms of androgen deficiency (decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, loss of axillary/pubic hair, muscle wasting) confirmed by **two separate fasting morning total testosterone (8:00–10:00 AM) measurements < 300 ng/dL** (10.4 nmol/L).
  - **Primary Hypogonadism (Testicular failure)**: Low testosterone with elevated LH and FSH (e.g., Klinefelter syndrome 47,XXY).
  - **Secondary Hypogonadism (Hypothalamic-Pituitary failure)**: Low testosterone with low or inappropriately normal LH/FSH (e.g., Kallmann syndrome, hyperprolactinemia).
- **Manual Test References**: `Testosterone` (P.563), `Luteinizing Hormone` (P.387), `Follicle-Stimulating Hormone` (P.267).

## 3. Gastrointestinal, Hepatic & Pancreatic Disorders

### 3.1 Acute Pancreatitis (Revised Atlanta Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Revised Atlanta Classification for Acute Pancreatitis.
- **Diagnostic Requirement**: Requires at least **2 of the following 3 features**:
  1. **Characteristic Abdominal Pain**: Severe epigastric pain radiating to the back;
  2. **Serum Amylase or Lipase**: Elevated **≥ 3 times the upper limit of normal** (Lipase is preferred due to higher sensitivity and prolonged elevation of 8–14 days);
  3. **Characteristic Imaging Findings**: Contrast-enhanced CT, MRI, or transabdominal ultrasound demonstrating pancreatic edema, peripancreatic fluid collections, or necrosis.
- **Manual Test References**: `Lipase` (P.381), `Amylase, Serum` (P.47), `Abdominal Sonogram` (P.19).

---

### 3.2 Colorectal Cancer Screening (ACS & USPSTF)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: USPSTF & American Cancer Society (ACS) Screening Guidelines.
- **Screening Recommendations for Average-Risk Adults**:
  - **Starting Age**: Age 45 to 75.
  - **Recommended Screening Modalities & Intervals**:
    - **Colonoscopy (Gold Standard)**: Every 10 years;
    - **Annual Fecal Immunochemical Test (FIT)**: Highly sensitive for human globin;
    - **Stool DNA-FIT (Cologuard)**: Every 3 years;
    - **Flexible Sigmoidoscopy**: Every 5 years;
    - **CT Colonography (Virtual Colonoscopy)**: Every 5 years.
- **Manual Test References**: `Colonoscopy` (P.193), `Fecal Occult Blood Test` (P.254).

---

### 3.3 Chronic Hepatitis B & C (AGA Guidelines)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: AASLD / AGA Practice Guidelines.
- **Hepatitis B Diagnostic Serologic Patterns**:
  - **Acute Infection**: Positive HBsAg, positive IgM anti-HBc;
  - **Chronic Infection**: Persistent **HBsAg positive > 6 months**, positive IgG anti-HBc;
  - **Vaccine Immunity**: Isolated positive anti-HBs (HBsAb); negative anti-HBc;
  - **Natural Infection Immunity**: Positive anti-HBs AND positive IgG anti-HBc.
- **Hepatitis C Diagnostic Sequence**:
  - Screening: HCV Antibody (anti-HCV);
  - Confirmation: Quantitative real-time HCV RNA PCR. Detectable HCV RNA indicates active viral replication and candidacy for direct-acting antivirals (DAAs).
- **Manual Test References**: `Hepatitis B Surface Antigen` (P.320), `Hepatitis C Virus Antibody` (P.325).

---

### 3.4 Autoimmune Hepatitis (AIH)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: International Autoimmune Hepatitis Group (IAIHG) Simplified Criteria.
- **Diagnostic Criteria Scoring System (Definite AIH requires ≥ 7 points)**:
  1. **Autoantibodies**: ANA or ASMA ≥ 1:40 (1 pt), ≥ 1:80 (2 pts); or Anti-LKM1 ≥ 1:40 (2 pts);
  2. **Serum IgG**: Normal upper limit (1 pt), > 1.10 times upper limit (2 pts);
  3. **Liver Histology**: Compatible with AIH (1 pt), typical interface hepatitis with lymphocytic/plasmacytic infiltrate (2 pts);
  4. **Absence of Viral Hepatitis**: Viral hepatitis serologies all negative (2 pts).
- **Manual Test References**: `Antinuclear Antibody Test` (P.70), `Anti-Smooth Muscle Antibody Test` (P.73), `Anti-Liver/Kidney Microsomal Antibody` (P.66).

---

### 3.5 Celiac Disease (Gluten-Sensitive Enteropathy)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACG / AGA Clinical Guidelines for the Diagnosis of Celiac Disease.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Decision Pathway**:
  1. **First-Line Serology**: Serum **IgA Anti-Tissue Transglutaminase (anti-tTG)** antibody while on a gluten-containing diet (sensitivity & specificity > 95%);
  2. **Total Serum IgA**: Concurrently measured to exclude selective IgA deficiency. If IgA deficient, measure IgG anti-deamidated gliadin peptides (DGP) or IgG anti-tTG;
  3. **Small Bowel Biopsy (Gold Standard)**: Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) with multiple duodenal biopsies showing intraepithelial lymphocytosis, crypt hyperplasia, and **villous blunting / atrophy (Marsh classification)**.
- **Manual Test References**: `Anti-Gliadin Antibodies` (P.64), `Endoscopy` (P.243).

---

### 3.6 Barrett Esophagus & GERD
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACG Clinical Practice Guidelines for Barrett's Esophagus.
- **Diagnostic Criteria**:
  - Requires upper endoscopy showing salmon-colored columnar mucosal extension ≥ 1 cm above the gastroesophageal junction;
  - **Histopathologic Gold Standard**: Biopsy confirming **specialized intestinal metaplasia with goblet cells** (Alcían blue staining positive).
  - In refractory GERD, 24-hour ambulatory esophageal pH impedance monitoring showing total acid exposure time (pH < 4) > 6% confirms pathologic reflux.
- **Manual Test References**: `Endoscopy` (P.243), `Esophageal Function Tests` (P.249).

---

### 3.7 Statin-Induced Hepatotoxicity Monitoring
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: National Lipid Association (NLA) Statin Safety Task Force Guidelines.
- **Monitoring & Discontinuation Criteria**:
  - Baseline ALT/AST measurement before initiating statin therapy;
  - **Threshold for Statin Interruption**: Persistent elevation of serum **ALT or AST > 3 times the upper limit of normal** on two consecutive occasions, or elevation associated with clinical jaundice, dark urine, and bilirubin elevation.
- **Manual Test References**: `Alanine Aminotransferase` (P.29), `Aspartate Aminotransferase` (P.97).

---

### 3.8 Persistent Infectious Diarrhea Evaluation
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: IDSA Practice Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Infectious Diarrhea.
- **Diagnostic Workup Indications**:
  - Diarrhea lasting > 7 days, accompanied by fever, severe abdominal pain, bloody stools (dysentery), tenesmus, or dehydration;
  - **First-Line Evaluation**: Stool culture for bacterial enteric pathogens (Salmonella, Shigella, Campylobacter, E. coli O157:H7), stool Shiga toxin assay, and Clostridioides difficile toxin PCR in hospitalized or antibiotic-exposed patients.
  - Stool Ova and Parasites (O&P) indicated in persistent diarrhea > 14 days or travel to endemic regions (Giardia, Cryptosporidium, Entamoeba histolytica).
- **Manual Test References**: `Stool Culture` (P.557), `Ova and Parasites Test` (P.450).

## 4. Renal & Genitourinary Disorders

### 4.1 Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD / KDIGO Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) 2012 Clinical Practice Guidelines.
- **Diagnostic Definition**: Abnormalities of kidney structure or function present for **> 3 months**, with implications for health, manifest by either:
  1. **GFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²** (CKD Stages G3a–G5); OR
  2. **Markers of Kidney Damage**: Albuminuria (AER ≥ 30 mg/24h; UACR ≥ 30 mg/g [≥ 3 mg/mmol]), urine sediment abnormalities (red cell casts, white cell casts), tubular disorders, histologic abnormalities, structural imaging abnormalities (polycystic kidneys), or history of kidney transplantation.
- **GFR Staging**: G1 (≥90), G2 (60–89), G3a (45–59), G3b (30–44), G4 (15–29), G5 (<15 or dialysis / kidney failure).
- **Manual Test References**: `Creatinine Clearance` (P.208), `Creatinine, Blood` (P.204), `Microalbuminuria` (P.415).

---

### 4.2 Urinary Tract Infection & Pyelonephritis
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: IDSA Guidelines for the Treatment of Uncomplicated Acute Cystitis and Pyelonephritis.
- **Microbiologic Diagnostic Thresholds**:
  - **Acute Uncomplicated Cystitis (Women with dysuria, urgency, frequency)**: Significant bacteriuria defined as **≥ 10³ CFU/mL** of a uropathogen (most commonly E. coli);
  - **Acute Pyelonephritis (Fever, flank pain, costovertebral angle tenderness)**: **≥ 10⁴ CFU/mL**;
  - **Catheter-Associated UTI**: **≥ 10⁵ CFU/mL** with signs/symptoms;
  - **Suprapubic Aspiration**: Any bacterial growth (≥ 10² CFU/mL) is diagnostic.
- **Manual Test References**: `Urine Culture and Sensitivity` (P.594), `Urinalysis` (P.591).

---

### 4.3 Ureterolithiasis & Nephrolithiasis
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: AUA / EAU Guidelines on Urolithiasis.
- **Diagnostic Imaging Standards**:
  - **Gold Standard**: Noncontrast Spiral CT of the abdomen and pelvis (CT Stone Protocol). Highly sensitive (> 98%) and specific for all stone compositions (including radiolucent uric acid stones, except indinavir stones);
  - **Renal Ultrasound**: Preferred initial modality in pregnant women and children (acoustic shadowing with twinkling artifact; evaluation for hydronephrosis).
- **Manual Test References**: `Computed Tomography` (P.195), `Kidney, Ureter, and Bladder Radiography` (P.21), `Renal Sonogram` (P.508).

---

### 4.4 Prostate Cancer Screening & Biopsy Indications (ACS)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: American Cancer Society (ACS) & AUA Early Detection Guidelines.
- **Biochemical Cutoffs & Biopsy Indications**:
  - **Serum Total PSA**:
    - < 4.0 ng/mL: Traditionally considered normal;
    - 4.0 – 10.0 ng/mL: Diagnostic gray zone (25% cancer risk; consider free-to-total PSA ratio: ratio < 10% indicates high cancer risk);
    - > 10.0 ng/mL: Strong cancer suspicion (> 50% positive biopsy rate; urgent prostate biopsy indicated).
  - **Prostate Biopsy**: Transrectal ultrasound-guided (TRUS) core needle biopsy is mandatory for definitive histologic confirmation (Gleason score grading).
- **Manual Test References**: `Prostate-Specific Antigen` (P.485).

## 5. Hematologic & Oncologic Disorders

### 5.1 Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC / ISTH Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) Overt DIC Scoring System.
- **Diagnostic Requirement**: Score **≥ 5 points** confirms overt DIC (in a patient with an underlying condition known to trigger DIC):
  1. **Platelet Count**: > 100,000/mcL (0 pts); 50,000–100,000/mcL (1 pt); < 50,000/mcL (2 pts);
  2. **Elevated Fibrin-Related Marker (D-dimer / FDP)**: No increase (0 pts); Moderate increase (2 pts); Strong increase (3 pts);
  3. **Prolonged Prothrombin Time (PT)**: < 3 sec prolongation (0 pts); 3–6 sec prolongation (1 pt); > 6 sec prolongation (2 pts);
  4. **Fibrinogen Level**: ≥ 1.0 g/L (0 pts); < 1.0 g/L (1 pt).
- **Manual Test References**: `Platelet Count` (P.472), `D-Dimer Test` (P.228), `Prothrombin Time` (P.488), `Fibrinogen` (P.262).

---

### 5.2 Multiple Myeloma (CRAB Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) Diagnostic Criteria.
- **Diagnostic Requirement**: Clonal bone marrow plasma cells ≥ 10% or biopsy-proven bony/extramedullary plasmacytoma, PLUS at least one **CRAB feature**:
  - **C (Hypercalcemia)**: Serum calcium > 11 mg/dL (> 0.25 mmol/L above normal);
  - **R (Renal Insufficiency)**: Serum creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL (or creatinine clearance < 40 mL/min);
  - **A (Anemia)**: Hemoglobin < 10 g/dL (or > 2 g/dL below lower limit of normal);
  - **B (Bone Lesions)**: ≥ 1 osteolytic lesions on skeletal radiography, CT, or PET-CT.
  - **Myeloma-Defining Biomarkers**: Clonal bone marrow plasma cells ≥ 60%; involved/uninvolved serum free light chain ratio ≥ 100; > 1 focal MRI lesion.
- **Manual Test References**: `Protein Electrophoresis, Serum` (P.481), `Bence Jones Protein` (P.104), `Bone Marrow Biopsy` (P.120).

---

### 5.3 Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT / 4T Score)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Warkentin 4T Scoring System for HIT.
- **4T Clinical Probability Scoring**:
  - **Thrombocytopenia**: Fall in platelets > 50% and nadir ≥ 20,000/mcL (2 pts); Fall 30–50% (1 pt); Fall < 30% (0 pts);
  - **Timing of platelet fall**: Days 5–10 post-heparin onset (2 pts); Day > 10 or rapid fall with recent heparin exposure (1–2 pts);
  - **Thrombosis or other sequelae**: New confirmed thrombosis, skin necrosis, or systemic reaction (2 pts); Progressive/recurrent thrombosis (1 pt);
  - **oTher causes for thrombocytopenia**: None apparent (2 pts); Possible (1 pt); Definite (0 pts).
  - **Probability**: 0–3 points: Low probability (HIT ruled out); 4–5: Intermediate; 6–8: High probability (immediately stop all heparin, start non-heparin direct thrombin inhibitor [Argatroban / Bivalirudin]).
- **Manual Test References**: `Platelet Count` (P.472), `Heparin Anti-Platelet Factor 4 Antibody` (P.317).

---

### 5.4 Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: WHO & American Society of Hematology (ASH) Diagnostic Guidelines.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Iron Panel**:
  - **Serum Ferritin**: **< 15–30 mcg/L** (most sensitive and specific single biochemical marker; values < 15 mcg/L are diagnostic of total iron depletion). Note: Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant and may be falsely normal/elevated in infection or chronic inflammation.
  - **Transferrin Saturation (TSAT)**: Serum iron / TIBC × 100 < 16–20%;
  - **Total Iron-Binding Capacity (TIBC)**: Elevated (> 400 mcg/dL);
  - **Serum Iron**: Low (< 50 mcg/dL);
  - **RBC Indices**: Microcytic hypochromic (MCV < 80 fL, MCHC < 32 g/dL), elevated RDW.
- **Manual Test References**: `Iron and Total Iron-Binding Capacity` (P.372), `Ferritin` (P.259), `Complete Blood Count` (P.194).

---

### 5.5 Megaloblastic Anemia: Vitamin B12 vs. Folate Deficiency
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: British Society for Haematology (BSH) Guidelines on Cobalamin and Folate Disorders.
- **Biochemical Differentiation Matrix**:
  - **Common Features**: Macrocytic anemia (MCV > 100 fL, often > 115 fL), hypersegmented neutrophils (≥ 5 lobes in > 5% of cells), elevated serum LDH and indirect bilirubin.
  - **Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) Deficiency**:
    - Low serum B12 (< 200 pg/mL);
    - **Methylmalonic Acid (MMA)**: **Elevated** (pathognomonic; distinguishes B12 from folate deficiency);
    - **Homocysteine**: **Elevated**;
    - Clinical hallmark: Subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord (dorsal and lateral columns).
  - **Folate Deficiency**:
    - Low serum folate (< 2–4 ng/mL) and low RBC folate;
    - **Methylmalonic Acid (MMA)**: **Normal**;
    - **Homocysteine**: **Elevated**;
    - No neurologic symptoms.
- **Manual Test References**: `Vitamin B12` (P.603), `Folate` (P.265), `Methylmalonic Acid` (P.413).

---

### 5.6 Antithrombin Deficiency & Hereditary Thrombophilia
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: British Committee for Standards in Haematology (BCSH) Guidelines on Thrombophilia Testing.
- **Diagnostic Criteria**:
  - Functional Antithrombin activity assay < 80% (indicates hereditary or acquired deficiency);
  - **Clinical Testing pearl**: Testing should not be performed during acute thrombotic events or while on heparin/warfarin therapy due to acquired consumption and drug-induced artifact.
- **Manual Test References**: `Antithrombin III` (P.78).

---

### 5.7 Breast Cancer Screening, BRCA Testing & Biopsy Indications
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: USPSTF & NCCN Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment Guidelines.
- **Screening & Workup Rules**:
  - **Mammography**: Biennial screening mammography for women aged 40/50 to 74 years;
  - **BRCA 1/2 Genetic Testing**: Recommended in personal or family history of early-onset breast cancer (< 50 years), bilateral breast cancer, ovarian cancer, or male breast cancer.
  - **BI-RADS Biopsy Indication**: BI-RADS Category 4 (suspicious) or Category 5 (highly suggestive of malignancy) mandates ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy.
- **Manual Test References**: `Mammography` (P.402), `BRCA 1/2` (P.129), `Breast Biopsy` (P.130).

---

### 5.8 Small Cell & Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Staging
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: IASLC / AJCC 8th Edition Staging, VALSG Lung Cancer Staging.
- **Diagnostic Classification**:
  - **Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC)**:
    - Limited Stage: Confined to one hemithorax and regional lymph nodes encompassed in a single tolerable radiotherapy port;
    - Extensive Stage: Distant metastases or disease extending beyond limited-stage boundaries.
  - **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**: TNM staging based on primary tumor size, nodal stations, and distant metastases (PET-CT and brain MRI).
- **Manual Test References**: `Chest Radiography` (P.181), `Computed Tomography` (P.195), `Bronchoscopy` (P.134).

---

### 5.9 Pancreatic & Ovarian Cancer Tumor Marker Guidelines
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ASCO & USPSTF Guidelines on Tumor Biomarkers.
- **Evidence-Based Marker Guidelines**:
  - **CA 19-9 (Pancreatic Cancer)**: Not recommended for population screening (poor sensitivity in Lewis-negative individuals and falsely elevated in benign biliary obstruction). Indicated for assessing resectability and monitoring therapy.
  - **CA-125 (Ovarian Cancer)**: USPSTF Grade D recommendation (recommends AGAINST routine screening in asymptomatic average-risk women; lacks mortality benefit and causes false-positive laparotomies). Indicated for monitoring response to therapy in diagnosed ovarian carcinoma.
- **Manual Test References**: `CA 19-9` (P.137), `CA-125` (P.138).

## 6. Infectious Diseases

### 6.1 Tuberculosis & Mantoux TST / PPD Criteria
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: CDC / ATS / IDSA Targeted Tuberculin Testing and Treatment of Latent TB Guidelines.
- **PPD Induration Diagnostic Thresholds (measured 48–72 hours post-injection)**:
  - **≥ 5 mm**: Positive in HIV-infected patients, recent close contacts of active TB cases, patients with fibrotic changes on chest radiograph, and organ transplant recipients / immunosuppressed (taking ≥15 mg/day prednisone for ≥ 1 month);
  - **≥ 10 mm**: Positive in recent immigrants (< 5 years) from high-prevalence countries, IV drug users, healthcare workers, prison inmates, children < 4 years, and patients with chronic high-risk conditions (silicosis, diabetes, CKD, hematologic malignancies);
  - **≥ 15 mm**: Positive in any person with no known risk factors for TB.
- **Definitive Diagnosis of Active TB**: Sputum Acid-Fast Bacilli (AFB) smear and mycobacterial culture (Gold Standard).
- **Manual Test References**: `Tuberculin Skin Test` (P.589), `Acid-Fast Bacilli` (P.23).

---

### 6.2 HIV Infection & AIDS (CDC Definition)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: CDC Revised Surveillance Case Definition for HIV Infection.
- **Diagnostic Criteria**:
  - **Fourth-Generation Screening**: HIV-1/2 antigen/antibody combination immunoassay (detects HIV-1 p24 antigen and HIV-1/2 antibodies).
  - **Confirmatory Testing**: HIV-1/2 differentiation immunoassay; if indeterminate, qualitative HIV-1 RNA nucleic acid testing (NAT).
  - **AIDS Case Definition**: Confirmed HIV infection PLUS either:
    1. **CD4+ T-lymphocyte count < 200 cells/mcL** (or CD4 percentage < 14%); OR
    2. Presence of an **AIDS-defining opportunistic infection / condition** (e.g., Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia, Cryptococcal meningitis, Kaposi sarcoma, CMV retinitis, Toxoplasmosis, Primary CNS lymphoma).
- **Manual Test References**: `HIV Antibody Test` (P.332), `CD4/CD8 Count` (P.164), `Viral Load Test` (P.605).

---

### 6.3 Syphilis Serology: Screening & Confirmatory Testing
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: CDC Sexually Transmitted Infections Treatment Guidelines.
- **Diagnostic Algorithms (Traditional vs. Reverse Sequence)**:
  1. **Nontreponemal Tests (RPR / VDRL)**: Reflect disease activity and response to treatment (four-fold change in titer required for clinically significant rise or fall). High false-positive rate (SLE, pregnancy, leprosy, malaria).
  2. **Treponemal Tests (FTA-ABS, TP-PA, EIA)**: Qualitative confirmatory tests. Once positive, remain positive for life regardless of cure.
  3. **Neurosyphilis Confirmation**: CSF VDRL (highly specific; diagnostic of neurosyphilis when reactive in absence of blood contamination).
- **Manual Test References**: `VDRL Test` (P.602), `Fluorescent Treponemal Antibody-Absorption` (P.264).

---

### 6.4 Lyme Disease: Two-Tiered Serologic Criteria
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: CDC / IDSA Recommendations for the Serologic Diagnosis of Lyme Disease.
- **Two-Tiered Testing Algorithm**:
  1. **First Tier**: Sensitive enzyme immunoassay (EIA) or immunofluorescence assay (IFA) for Borrelia burgdorferi. If negative, no further testing needed.
  2. **Second Tier**: Western Blot (Immunoblot):
     - **IgM Western Blot**: Positive if **≥ 2 of 3 bands** are present (24, 39, 41 kDa); valid only in the first 4 weeks of infection;
     - **IgG Western Blot**: Positive if **≥ 5 of 10 bands** are present (18, 23, 28, 30, 39, 41, 45, 58, 66, 93 kDa); required for manifestations lasting > 4 weeks (Lyme arthritis, encephalopathy).
  - **Clinical Pearl**: Erythema migrans rash in an endemic area is diagnostic on its own; serologic testing is not required and will often be negative early on.
- **Manual Test References**: `Lyme Disease Test` (P.390).

---

### 6.5 Group A Streptococcal Pharyngitis (Centor Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Centor Criteria & IDSA Pharyngitis Guidelines.
- **Centor Scoring System (1 point each)**:
  - History of fever (> 38.0°C / 100.4°F);
  - Absence of cough;
  - Tender anterior cervical adenopathy;
  - Tonsillar exudates or swelling;
  - Age: 3–14 years (+1), 15–44 years (0), ≥ 45 years (-1).
- **Clinical Management**:
  - Score 0–1: No antibiotic or testing needed (viral etiology);
  - Score 2–3: Rapid Antigen Detection Test (RADT) or throat culture;
  - Score 4: Empirical antibiotic treatment or rapid testing.
- **Manual Test References**: `Throat Culture` (P.566), `Antistreptolysin-O Titer` (P.77).

---

### 6.6 Infectious Mononucleosis (Monospot Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: CDC Clinical Diagnosis of Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) Mononucleosis.
- **Diagnostic Triad & Confirmation**:
  - Clinical triad: Fever, pharyngitis, and posterior cervical lymphadenopathy (often with splenomegaly);
  - **Peripheral Blood Smear**: Lymphocytosis (> 50%) with **> 10% atypical lymphocytes** (reactive CD8+ cytotoxic T cells);
  - **Heterophile Antibody Test (Monospot)**: Positive in > 90% of adults (may be false negative in first week or in children < 4 years; confirm with EBV viral capsid antigen [VCA-IgM] if negative).
- **Manual Test References**: `Heterophile Antibodies` (P.330), `Epstein-Barr Virus Antibodies` (P.247).

---

### 6.7 Bacterial Vaginosis (Amsel Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Amsel Clinical Criteria for Bacterial Vaginosis.
- **Diagnostic Requirement**: Presence of **≥ 3 of the following 4 criteria**:
  1. Homogeneous, thin, gray-white discharge that smoothly coats vaginal walls;
  2. Vaginal pH > 4.5;
  3. Positive Whiff test (fishy amine odor on addition of 10% potassium hydroxide [KOH]);
  4. **Clue Cells** on saline wet mount (> 20% of vaginal epithelial cells studded with adherent coccobacilli, obscuring cell borders).
- **Manual Test References**: `Vaginal Smear` (P.598).

---

### 6.8 Chlamydia & Gonorrhea Screening (USPSTF)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: USPSTF & CDC STI Screening Guidelines.
- **Screening Recommendations**:
  - Annual screening for Chlamydia trachomatis and Neisseria gonorrhoeae in all sexually active women aged ≤ 24 years, and in older women at increased risk.
  - **Gold Standard Diagnostic Test**: Nucleic Acid Amplification Testing (**NAAT**) on first-catch urine or vaginal/endocervical swabs (highest sensitivity and specificity).
- **Manual Test References**: `Chlamydia Test` (P.185), `Gonorrhea Culture` (P.290).

---

### 6.9 Genital Herpes Simplex Virus & Delivery Guidelines
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACOG Practice Bulletin on Management of Genital Herpes in Pregnancy.
- **Clinical Delivery Indications**:
  - **Cesarean Delivery Indication**: Mandated in women with **active genital HSV lesions or prodromal symptoms** (vulvar burning, tingling) at the onset of labor to prevent neonatal transmission;
  - In women without active genital lesions or prodromal symptoms at labor, vaginal delivery is recommended.
- **Manual Test References**: `Herpes Simplex Virus Antibodies` (P.328).

## 7. Rheumatologic & Autoimmune Disorders

### 7.1 Rheumatoid Arthritis (ACR Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: 2010 ACR / EULAR Rheumatoid Arthritis Classification Criteria.
- **Diagnostic Criteria Scoring (Definite RA requires ≥ 6 of 10 points)**:
  1. **Joint Involvement**: 1 large joint (0 pts); 2–10 large joints (1 pt); 1–3 small joints (2 pts); 4–10 small joints (3 pts); > 10 joints including ≥ 1 small joint (5 pts);
  2. **Serology**: Negative RF and negative ACPA (0 pts); Low-positive RF or ACPA (2 pts); High-positive RF or ACPA (3 pts);
  3. **Acute-Phase Reactants**: Normal CRP and normal ESR (0 pts); Abnormal CRP or abnormal ESR (1 pt);
  4. **Duration of Symptoms**: < 6 weeks (0 pts); ≥ 6 weeks (1 pt).
  - **Anti-Cyclic Citrullinated Peptide (Anti-CCP / ACPA)**: Most specific marker (> 95% specificity); predictive of aggressive, erosive joint destruction.
- **Manual Test References**: `Rheumatoid Factor` (P.516), `Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate` (P.245), `C-Reactive Protein` (P.200).

---

### 7.2 Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE / ACR Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACR / SLICC Revised Criteria for Classification of SLE.
- **ACR 11 Classic Criteria (Requires ≥ 4 criteria)**:
  1. Malar rash; 2. Discoid rash; 3. Photosensitivity; 4. Oral ulcers; 5. Nonerosive arthritis (≥2 joints); 6. Serositis (pleuritis or pericarditis); 7. Renal disorder (persistent proteinuria > 0.5 g/day or cellular casts); 8. Neurologic disorder (seizures or psychosis); 9. Hematologic disorder (hemolytic anemia, leukopenia < 4,000, lymphopenia < 1,500, or thrombocytopenia < 100,000); 10. Immunologic disorder (Anti-dsDNA, Anti-Smith, or Antiphospholipid antibody); 11. Antinuclear antibody (ANA positive).
- **Manual Test References**: `Antinuclear Antibody Test` (P.70), `Anti-DNA Antibody Test` (P.63), `Complement` (P.191).

---

### 7.3 Gout vs. Pseudogout: Synovial Crystal Differentiation
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACR Guidelines for the Management of Gout.
- **Arthrocentesis Synovial Fluid Differential Matrix**:
  - **Gout (Monosodium Urate Crystals)**:
    - Morphology: Needle-shaped, pointed ends;
    - Polarized Light Microscopy: **Strongly negative birefringence**;
    - Compensator Color: Yellow when parallel to the slow axis; blue when perpendicular.
  - **Pseudogout / CPPD (Calcium Pyrophosphate Dihydrate Crystals)**:
    - Morphology: Rhomboid-shaped, rectangular;
    - Polarized Light Microscopy: **Weakly positive birefringence**;
    - Compensator Color: Blue when parallel to the slow axis; yellow when perpendicular;
    - Chondrocalcinosis on plain radiography.
- **Manual Test References**: `Arthrocentesis` (P.92), `Uric Acid, Blood` (P.590).

---

### 7.4 Myasthenia Gravis (MG)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Practice Parameter for Evaluation of Myasthenia Gravis.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Modalities**:
  1. **Serologic Testing**: Serum **Acetylcholine Receptor Antibodies (AChR-Ab)**: positive in 85–90% of generalized MG (highly specific). If negative, test Muscle-Specific Kinase Antibodies (Anti-MuSK);
  2. **Electrophysiologic Testing**: Repetitive nerve stimulation (RNS) demonstrating significant decremental compound muscle action potential (> 10% decrease);
  3. **Edrophonium (Tensilon) Test**: Rapid, transient improvement in muscle weakness following IV edrophonium (short-acting acetylcholinesterase inhibitor; atropine must be at bedside).
- **Manual Test References**: `Acetylcholine Receptor Antibodies` (P.22), `Tensilon Test` (P.562).

## 8. Respiratory & Sleep Disorders

### 8.1 COPD Exacerbation & Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) Indications
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) Guidelines.
- **Arterial Blood Gas Indications in COPD**:
  - Indicated in all hospitalized patients with acute exacerbation, altered mental status, or pulse oximetry saturation < 92%.
  - **Respiratory Failure Thresholds**:
    - **Hypoxemic Failure**: PaO2 < 60 mmHg (8.0 kPa) on ambient air;
    - **Hypercapnic Acidosis Failure**: PaCO2 > 45–50 mmHg with arterial pH < 7.35; triggers indication for Noninvasive Positive Pressure Ventilation (NIV / BiPAP).
- **Manual Test References**: `Arterial Blood Gases` (P.85).

---

### 8.2 Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency (AATD)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ATS / ERS Statement on Standards for the Diagnosis of AATD.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Laboratory Thresholds**:
  - **Indications**: Early-onset panacinar emphysema (< 45 years, lower lobes predominant, minimal or non-smoker) or unexplained neonatal cholestasis / cirrhosis;
  - **Serum AAT Quantification**: Serum AAT concentration **< 50–80 mg/dL** (< 11 umol/L; protective threshold);
  - **Phenotyping & Genotyping**: Isoelectric focusing demonstrating homozygous Pi*ZZ genotype (severe deficiency) or Pi*MZ.
- **Manual Test References**: `Alpha1-Antitrypsin Test` (P.37).

---

### 8.3 Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA / ASDA Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: AASM / ASDA Practice Parameters for Polysomnography.
- **Diagnostic Polysomnography Criteria (Apnea-Hypopnea Index [AHI])**:
  - Diagnosis established if **AHI ≥ 5 events/hour** with documented symptoms (excessive daytime sleepiness, loud snoring, witnessed apneas, nocturnal gasping, hypertension), OR **AHI ≥ 15 events/hour** regardless of symptoms;
  - **Severity Stratification**:
    - Mild: AHI 5–14.9;
    - Moderate: AHI 15–29.9;
    - Severe: AHI ≥ 30 events/hour.
- **Manual Test References**: `Polysomnography` (P.477).

## 9. Neurological, Musculoskeletal & Trauma

### 9.1 CNS Infections & CSF Differential Diagnosis Matrix
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: IDSA Practice Guidelines for Healthcare-Associated Ventriculitis and Meningitis.
- **Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) Lumbar Puncture Differentiation**:
  - **Bacterial Meningitis**: Elevated opening pressure (> 200–300 mm H2O); marked pleocytosis (> 1,000/mcL) with **neutrophil predominance (> 80%)**; markedly elevated protein (> 100–500 mg/dL); markedly decreased glucose (< 40 mg/dL, CSF-to-serum glucose ratio < 0.4);
  - **Viral (Aseptic) Meningitis**: Normal/mildly elevated opening pressure; moderate pleocytosis (10–500/mcL) with **lymphocytic predominance**; normal or mildly elevated protein (< 100 mg/dL); normal glucose (CSF-to-serum glucose ratio > 0.6);
  - **Fungal / Cryptococcal Meningitis**: Elevated opening pressure; lymphocytic pleocytosis; elevated protein; decreased glucose; positive India ink preparation / Cryptococcal antigen;
  - **Tuberculous Meningitis**: Markedly elevated opening pressure; lymphocytic pleocytosis; very high protein (> 100–500 mg/dL); very low glucose (< 30 mg/dL); cobweb clot formation.
- **Manual Test References**: `Lumbar Puncture` (P.385).

---

### 9.2 Ottawa Ankle & Foot Rules
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: Stiell et al., Emergency Medicine Ottawa Ankle Rules.
- **Radiography Indications (High sensitivity > 98% to exclude fractures)**:
  - **Ankle X-ray Series Indicated** if there is pain in the malleolar zone AND any of the following:
    1. Bone tenderness along distal 6 cm of posterior edge or tip of lateral malleolus;
    2. Bone tenderness along distal 6 cm of posterior edge or tip of medial malleolus;
    3. Inability to bear weight both immediately after injury and in the emergency department (4 steps).
  - **Foot X-ray Series Indicated** if there is pain in the midfoot zone AND any of the following:
    1. Bone tenderness at the base of the 5th metatarsal;
    2. Bone tenderness at the navicular bone;
    3. Inability to bear weight both immediately and in the ED (4 steps).
- **Manual Test References**: `X-Ray, Skeletal` (P.620).

---

### 9.3 Osteoporosis & Osteopenia (WHO DEXA Criteria)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: World Health Organization (WHO) & NOF Guidelines.
- **Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry (DEXA) T-Score Classification**:
  - Measured at femoral neck, total hip, or lumbar spine:
    - **Normal**: T-score **≥ -1.0 SD**;
    - **Osteopenia (Low Bone Mass)**: T-score between **-1.0 and -2.5 SD**;
    - **Osteoporosis**: T-score **≤ -2.5 SD**;
    - **Severe (Established) Osteoporosis**: T-score ≤ -2.5 SD in the presence of one or more fragility fractures.
  - **Screening Indication**: All women aged ≥ 65 years and men aged ≥ 70 years.
- **Manual Test References**: `Bone Mineral Density` (P.122).

## 10. Obstetrics, Gynecology & Prenatal Screening

### 10.1 Rh Isoimmunization Screening & Prophylaxis
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACOG Practice Bulletin on Prevention of Rh D Alloimmunization.
- **Screening & Prevention Protocol**:
  - All pregnant women should have ABO blood group and Rh(D) typing with indirect Coombs antibody screen at first prenatal visit.
  - In unsensitized Rh(D)-negative women:
    - Prophylactic administration of **300 mcg Rh0(D) immune globulin (RhoGAM) at 28 weeks of gestation**;
    - Administration of a second dose within **72 hours postpartum** if the infant is confirmed Rh(D) positive.
  - **Kleihauer-Betke Test**: Quantifies volume of fetomaternal hemorrhage to determine additional doses of RhoGAM needed after maternal trauma or delivery.
- **Manual Test References**: `Blood Typing` (P.117), `Coombs' Test, Indirect` (P.197), `Kleihauer-Betke Test` (P.378).

---

### 10.2 Neural Tube Defects & Aneuploidy Prenatal Screening (MSAFP & Triple Screen)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACOG Practice Guidelines for Prenatal Screening.
- **Maternal Serum Alpha-Fetoprotein (MSAFP) Interpretation (15–20 weeks gestation)**:
  - **Elevated MSAFP (> 2.0–2.5 MoM [Multiples of the Median])**: Open neural tube defects (anencephaly, spina bifida), abdominal wall defects (omphalocele, gastroschisis), multifetal gestation, underestimation of gestational age, fetal demise.
  - **Decreased MSAFP (< 0.5 MoM)**: Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome), Trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome), overestimation of gestational age.
  - **Triple Screen Pattern for Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21)**: Decreased MSAFP, decreased unconjugated estriol (uE3), and **markedly elevated hCG**.
- **Manual Test References**: `Alpha-Fetoprotein` (P.38), `Triple Marker Test` (P.585).

---

### 10.3 Amniocentesis Indications (ACOG Guidelines)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: ACOG Practice Bulletin on Invasive Prenatal Diagnostic Testing.
- **Diagnostic Indications & Fetal Lung Maturity Indices**:
  - Indications: Advanced maternal age (≥ 35 years at delivery), abnormal noninvasive aneuploidy screening, family history of genetic disorders, assessment of fetal infection.
  - **Fetal Lung Maturity (Late Pregnancy Amniotic Fluid)**:
    - **Lecithin/Sphingomyelin (L/S) Ratio**: L/S ratio **≥ 2.0** (≥ 2.5 in diabetic mothers) confirms fetal lung maturity and minimal risk of Neonatal Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS);
    - **Phosphatidylglycerol (PG)**: Presence of PG confirms mature pulmonary surfactant production.
- **Manual Test References**: `Amniocentesis` (P.45).

---

### 10.4 Cervical Cancer Screening (ACOG & USPSTF Guidelines)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: USPSTF & ACOG Cervical Cancer Screening Guidelines.
- **Screening Schedule for Average-Risk Women**:
  - **Age < 21**: Screening not recommended regardless of sexual activity;
  - **Age 21–29**: Cervical cytology (Pap smear) alone every 3 years (HPV co-testing not recommended);
  - **Age 30–65**: Cervical cytology alone every 3 years, OR high-risk HPV (hrHPV) alone every 5 years, OR **hrHPV co-testing with cytology every 5 years** (preferred);
  - **Age > 65**: Discontinue screening if adequate prior negative screening (3 consecutive negative Pap tests or 2 negative co-tests within past 10 years).
- **Manual Test References**: `Papanicolaou Smear` (P.455), `Human Papillomavirus Test` (P.334).

## 11. Pediatrics & Inborn Errors of Metabolism

### 11.1 Phenylketonuria (PKU) Newborn Screening (AAFP Guidelines)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: AAFP & AAP Newborn Screening Guidelines.
- **Screening Protocol & Diagnostic Cutoffs**:
  - **Guthrie Bacterial Inhibition Assay / Tandem Mass Spectrometry (MS/MS)** on heel-prick blood spot collected after 24–48 hours of age following protein/milk ingestion.
  - **Diagnostic Confirmation**: Persistent plasma phenylalanine **> 20 mg/dL (1,200 umol/L)** with normal or low plasma tyrosine, resulting from phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH) deficiency or tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) cofactor deficiency.
- **Manual Test References**: `Phenylalanine, Blood` (P.466).

---

### 11.2 Child Sexual Abuse Forensic Evaluation (AAP Guidelines)
- **Authoritative Diagnostic Criteria & Guideline Source**: AAP Committee on Child Abuse and Neglect Practice Guidelines.
- **Diagnostic & Microbiologic Confirmation**:
  - Isolation of **Neisseria gonorrhoeae** by culture, or **Chlamydia trachomatis** by verified NAAT, or positive serology for **Treponema pallidum (Syphilis)** or **HIV** in a prepubertal child (beyond neonatal transmission period) constitutes definitive medical evidence of sexual contact and mandated reporting.
- **Manual Test References**: `Gonorrhea Culture` (P.290), `Chlamydia Test` (P.185), `Vaginal Smear` (P.598).
