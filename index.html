Question 01 of 20 Cardiology Myocardial Infarction

A 58-year-old male with a 25-pack-year smoking history and type 2 diabetes mellitus presents to the emergency department experiencing severe retrosternal chest pressure that radiates down his left arm. Vital signs are: BP 152/94 mm Hg, pulse 104/min, and oxygen saturation 96%. A 12-lead ECG demonstrates 3.5-mm ST-segment elevations in leads II, III, and aVF with reciprocal ST depressions in leads I and aVL.

troubleshoot
Which coronary artery is acutely occluded in this patient, and which cardiac conduction structure is most vulnerable to ischemia?
A Left anterior descending artery; Purkinje fiber arborization
B Right coronary artery; Atrioventricular (AV) node
check_circle
C Left circumflex artery; Sinoatrial (SA) node
D Right marginal artery; Right bundle branch
E Left main coronary artery; Left anterior fascicle
check_circle Correct! High-Yield Concept Mastered

Educational Objective: ST elevations in leads II, III, and aVF identify an acute inferior wall myocardial infarction caused by right coronary artery (RCA) occlusion in right-dominant individuals (~85-90% of population). The RCA supplies the posterior descending artery (PDA) and the AV nodal branch, rendering the AV node highly vulnerable to bradyarrhythmias and AV block.

Question 02 of 20 Pharmacology Cardiac Glycosides

A 63-year-old female with atrial fibrillation and chronic kidney disease (eGFR 24 mL/min) is taking digoxin for rate control. She presents complaining of nausea, palpitations, and blurred yellow-tinted vision. ECG shows paroxysmal atrial tachycardia with 2:1 AV block. Laboratory testing confirms acute digitalis toxicity.

troubleshoot
What cellular alteration directly accounts for the increased cardiac automaticity and delayed afterdepolarizations (DADs) observed in this patient?
A Uncoupling of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation
B Excessive effluence of intracellular potassium via IKr channels
cancel
C Selective activation of funny (If) inward sodium/potassium current
D Intracellular calcium overload triggering spontaneous sarcoplasmic reticulum release
check_circle
E Prolongation of phase 0 depolarization via direct voltage-gated Na+ blockade
cancel Incorrect (Your Selection: B | Key: D)

Educational Objective: Digoxin inhibits sarcolemmal Na+/K+-ATPase, elevating intracellular [Na+]. This reduces the forward extrusion gradient of the 3Na+/1Ca2+ antiporter (NCX), leading to marked cytosolic and sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) Ca2+ overload. Unstable diastolic calcium release generates transient inward currents (Iti), triggering delayed afterdepolarizations (DADs) and ventricular arrhythmias.
