### Question 1: Hypertensive Heart Disease: Molecular Mechanisms of Hypertrophy
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Core Concept / 重点考点**: Chronic pressure overload (e.g., hypertension) leads to left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) via activation of hypertrophic signaling pathways involving mechanical stress sensors, G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), tyrosine kinases, and transcription factors, ultimately increasing protein synthesis and altering gene expression (e.g., fetal gene program). (慢性压力负荷导致心肌肥厚，通过激活涉及机械应力传感器、GPCR、酪氨酸激酶和转录因子的肥厚信号通路，最终增加蛋白质合成并改变基因表达。)

#### Clinical Vignette:
A 58-year-old male with a 30-year history of poorly controlled essential hypertension presents to his cardiologist complaining of increasing exertional dyspnea and fatigue over the past 6 months. His blood pressure today is 185/105 mmHg. Physical examination reveals a sustained apical impulse displaced laterally and inferiorly, and a grade III/VI systolic ejection murmur heard best at the left sternal border. An echocardiogram shows a left ventricular wall thickness of 1.8 cm (normal < 1.1 cm) with preserved systolic function (ejection fraction 55%) and diastolic dysfunction. Histological examination of a myocardial biopsy reveals increased size of individual cardiomyocytes with enlarged nuclei and increased myofibrillar density. Which of the following molecular mechanisms is MOST directly responsible for the increased protein synthesis observed in this patient's hypertrophied cardiomyocytes?

(A) Decreased activity of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway due to reduced insulin signaling.
(B) Increased activation of the Ras/MAPK pathway stimulated by mechanical stretch and growth factors.
(C) Upregulation of histone deacetylases (HDACs) leading to chromatin condensation and reduced transcription.
(D) Downregulation of the ubiquitin-proteasome system, impairing the degradation of damaged proteins.
(E) Increased expression of microRNAs (miRNAs) that target mRNAs encoding ribosomal proteins.

#### Correct Answer: B

#### High-Yield Pathophysiological Explanation:
Chronic pressure overload, as seen in long-standing hypertension, causes mechanical stress on cardiomyocytes. This stress activates various signaling pathways that promote hypertrophy. Mechanical stretch activates mechanosensitive ion channels and G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) like angiotensin II receptors (AT1R) and endothelin receptors. Activation of these receptors leads to the activation of small GTPases like Ras, which in turn activates the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway (also known as the Ras/MAPK pathway). The MAPK pathway ultimately activates transcription factors like c-Fos and c-Jun (AP-1 complex) and Elk-1 (Serum Response Factor, SRF), which increase the transcription of genes encoding structural proteins (e.g., myosin heavy chains, β-myosin), ribosomal proteins, and other components necessary for increased protein synthesis and cell growth. This pathway is a key mediator of cardiomyocyte hypertrophy in response to pressure overload. First Aid 2025 and Robbins Pathology emphasize the role of hypertrophic signaling cascades, including GPCR activation and MAPK pathways, in mediating cardiac hypertrophy.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- (A): The PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway is generally *upregulated* in hypertrophy, promoting protein synthesis and cell growth, not decreased. Reduced insulin signaling can occur in heart failure but is not the primary driver of hypertrophy in hypertension.
- (C): Histone deacetylases (HDACs) are generally *repressed* during hypertrophy, leading to chromatin decondensation and *increased* transcription of hypertrophic genes, not decreased.
- (D): The ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) is typically *overwhelmed* or its activity may be *altered* in hypertrophy, but its primary role is protein degradation. While impaired degradation can contribute to the accumulation of proteins, the *initial* stimulus for increased protein synthesis involves signaling pathways like MAPK, not downregulation of the UPS itself.
- (E): MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are involved in regulating gene expression, including hypertrophic genes. However, miRNAs typically *repress* translation by targeting mRNAs. Increased expression of miRNAs targeting ribosomal proteins would *decrease* protein synthesis, which is contrary to what occurs in hypertrophy.

#### Educational Objective (USMLE High-Yield Takeaway):
Chronic pressure overload (like hypertension) activates signaling pathways, including the Ras/MAPK pathway, leading to increased transcription of genes involved in protein synthesis and cardiomyocyte growth, resulting in left ventricular hypertrophy. Understanding these molecular mechanisms is crucial for understanding the pathophysiology of hypertensive heart disease.

### Question 2: Postmenopausal Bleeding and Endometrial Changes
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Core Concept / 重点考点**: Unopposed estrogen stimulation in postmenopausal women leads to endometrial hyperplasia, increasing the risk of progression to atypical hyperplasia and endometrioid adenocarcinoma. (绝经后女性中无对抗的雌激素刺激导致子宫内膜增生，增加进展为非典型增生和内膜样腺癌的风险)

#### Clinical Vignette:
A 52-year-old woman presents to her gynecologist complaining of intermittent vaginal bleeding for the past 6 months. She is postmenopausal, having experienced her final menstrual period 3 years ago. Her medical history is significant for obesity (BMI 35 kg/m²) and type 2 diabetes mellitus, managed with metformin. She denies hormone replacement therapy. Physical examination reveals a slightly enlarged uterus. Transvaginal ultrasound shows a thickened endometrium (15 mm). An endometrial biopsy is performed. Microscopic examination reveals tightly packed endometrial glands with elongated, hyperchromatic nuclei and loss of polarity, along with a significantly increased gland-to-stroma ratio compared to normal proliferative endometria. There is no evidence of stromal invasion.

Which of the following pathophysiological mechanisms is most likely responsible for the observed endometrial changes and the associated risk of malignancy?

(A) Increased expression of progesterone receptors leading to stromal breakdown.
(B) Decreased aromatase activity in adipose tissue, resulting in reduced estrogen levels.
(C) Unopposed estrogen stimulation leading to continuous proliferation of the endometrium.
(D) Increased expression of tumor suppressor genes like p53, causing cellular senescence.
(E) Decreased activity of estrogen receptors in endometrial cells, leading to stromal dominance.

#### Correct Answer: C

#### High-Yield Pathophysiological Explanation:
The patient is a postmenopausal woman presenting with abnormal uterine bleeding and a thickened endometrium. Her obesity is a key risk factor. Adipose tissue contains aromatase, which converts androgens to estrogens. In postmenopausal women, ovarian estrogen production ceases, but peripheral conversion of androgens to estrogens (primarily estrone) in adipose tissue continues. In obese women, increased adipose tissue leads to higher levels of peripheral estrogen production. This excess estrogen stimulates the endometrium, causing it to proliferate continuously without the cyclical shedding normally regulated by progesterone (which is absent postmenopause). This unopposed estrogen stimulation leads to endometrial hyperplasia, characterized by an increased gland-to-stroma ratio and glandular crowding. The described microscopic findings (crowded glands, increased gland-to-stroma ratio, elongated hyperchromatic nuclei, loss of polarity) are consistent with endometrial hyperplasia, potentially progressing to atypical hyperplasia or endometrioid adenocarcinoma if the unopposed estrogen stimulation persists. Unopposed estrogen is the primary driver of this process.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- (A): Increased progesterone receptor expression would lead to progesterone's antiproliferative effects, counteracting estrogen's influence and preventing hyperplasia, not causing it. Stromal breakdown is not the primary feature of hyperplasia; rather, the stroma is typically reduced relative to the glands.
- (B): Increased aromatase activity in adipose tissue leads to *increased*, not decreased, estrogen levels, especially in obese postmenopausal women. This increased estrogen is the cause of the endometrial changes.
- (D): Increased expression of tumor suppressor genes like p53 would inhibit cell proliferation and potentially induce apoptosis, preventing hyperplasia and cancer development. The described changes represent uncontrolled proliferation, often associated with *loss* or inactivation of tumor suppressor genes.
- (E): Decreased estrogen receptor activity would lead to reduced endometrial proliferation and potentially stromal dominance, not the glandular proliferation and increased gland-to-stroma ratio seen in hyperplasia. The pathology is driven by *increased* estrogen signaling via estrogen receptors.

#### Educational Objective (USMLE High-Yield Takeaway):
Unopposed estrogen stimulation is the primary cause of endometrial hyperplasia and subsequent endometrioid adenocarcinoma, particularly in postmenopausal women and those with conditions leading to excess estrogen production or lack of progesterone effect (e.g., obesity, anovulation, estrogen-secreting tumors). This highlights the importance of progesterone's role in counter-regulating estrogen's proliferative effects on the endometrium.

### Question 3: Disuse Atrophy in Immobilization
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Core Concept / 重点考点**: Disuse atrophy, specifically in skeletal muscle following immobilization, involves activation of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway and autophagy, mediated by E3 ubiquitin ligases like MuRF1 and MAFbx (atrogin-1). (废用性肌萎缩，骨骼肌在固定后，涉及泛素-蛋白酶体途径和自噬的激活，由泛素连接酶如MuRF1和MAFbx（肥大霉素-1）介导。)

#### Clinical Vignette:
A 24-year-old male collegiate soccer player suffers a closed fracture of the mid-shaft of his left femur during a game. He undergoes open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) and is subsequently placed in a long-leg cast extending from the groin to the toes for 8 weeks to allow for bone healing. After cast removal, physical examination reveals significant atrophy of the quadriceps femoris muscle on the affected left leg compared to the contralateral side. Muscle biopsy performed 2 weeks post-cast removal shows decreased muscle fiber cross-sectional area and increased numbers of autophagosomes within the muscle fibers. Which of the following molecular pathways is MOST directly responsible for the observed muscle atrophy in this patient?

(A) Increased activity of the mTORC1 complex leading to enhanced protein synthesis.
(B) Decreased expression of Bcl-2 family proteins, promoting apoptosis.
(C) Activation of the calcineurin pathway, stimulating muscle hypertrophy.
(D) Upregulation of E3 ubiquitin ligases such as MuRF1 and MAFbx, targeting contractile proteins for degradation via the proteasome.
(E) Increased mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative phosphorylation, leading to increased energy expenditure.

#### Correct Answer: D

#### High-Yield Pathophysiological Explanation:
Disuse atrophy, as seen in this patient following prolonged immobilization, is characterized by a decrease in muscle mass and fiber size. The primary mechanism involves the degradation of muscle proteins. This process is significantly mediated by the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway (UPP). In response to inactivity, signaling pathways (e.g., decreased IGF-1/Akt/mTOR signaling, increased calcineurin signaling) lead to the upregulation of specific E3 ubiquitin ligases, notably MuRF1 (Muscle RING Finger 1) and MAFbx (Muscle Atrophy F-box), also known as atrogin-1. These ligases target key structural proteins of the muscle fiber, such as myosin heavy chains and actin, for ubiquitination. Polyubiquitinated proteins are then recognized and degraded by the 26S proteasome. Autophagy also contributes to muscle protein degradation during atrophy, but the UPP, particularly via MuRF1 and MAFbx, is considered a major driver of protein loss in disuse atrophy. Option (D) correctly identifies this key molecular mechanism.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- (A): Increased mTORC1 activity promotes protein synthesis and muscle hypertrophy, not atrophy. Disuse atrophy is associated with *decreased* mTORC1 activity due to reduced growth factor signaling (like IGF-1) and increased inhibitory signals.
- (B): While apoptosis can contribute to muscle cell loss in certain conditions (e.g., severe denervation), disuse atrophy primarily involves protein degradation within existing fibers, leading to a reduction in fiber size, rather than widespread cell death via apoptosis. Bcl-2 family proteins are anti-apoptotic; decreased expression would promote apoptosis, which is not the main mechanism here.
- (C): The calcineurin pathway is typically associated with muscle *atrophy* in certain contexts (e.g., denervation, glucocorticoid treatment), but it does so by *promoting* the activity of MuRF1 and MAFbx, leading to UPP activation. It does not directly stimulate hypertrophy. Furthermore, the primary driver described in disuse atrophy is the UPP activation itself, mediated by MuRF1/MAFbx.
- (E): Increased mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative phosphorylation are characteristic of muscle adaptation to exercise and increased energy demand, leading to hypertrophy or maintenance, not atrophy. Atrophy is often associated with decreased mitochondrial function and oxidative capacity.

#### Educational Objective (USMLE High-Yield Takeaway):
Disuse atrophy in skeletal muscle is primarily driven by the activation of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway (UPP), mediated by E3 ubiquitin ligases like MuRF1 and MAFbx, which target contractile proteins for degradation. Autophagy also contributes. This contrasts with hypertrophy, which involves increased protein synthesis via pathways like mTORC1.

### Question 4: Barrett Esophagus & Metaplasia Reprogramming
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Core Concept / 重点考点**: Metaplasia is a reversible change where one differentiated cell type is replaced by another differentiated cell type, often in response to chronic irritation. This involves stem cell reprogramming. Barrett esophagus is a metaplastic change in the distal esophagus predisposing to adenocarcinoma. (Metaplasia 是一个可逆的过程，其中一种分化细胞类型被另一种分化细胞类型所取代，通常是对慢性刺激的反应。这涉及干细胞的重编程。巴雷特食管是食管远端的一种化生性改变，容易发展为腺癌。)

#### Clinical Vignette:
A 48-year-old male presents to his primary care physician complaining of worsening heartburn and acid regurgitation over the past 10 years, symptoms that are now occurring daily despite over-the-counter antacid use. He has no significant past medical history other than occasional NSAID use for headaches. Upper endoscopy reveals a segment of the distal esophagus exhibiting salmon-pink, velvety mucosa extending proximally from the gastroesophageal junction. Biopsies taken from this area show columnar epithelium containing goblet cells, replacing the normal squamous lining. Which of the following cellular processes best describes the underlying mechanism leading to this histological finding?

(A) Dysplasia, characterized by loss of cellular differentiation and architectural disorganization.
(B) Aplasia, characterized by the failure of an organ or tissue to develop.
(C) Hyperplasia, characterized by an increase in the number of cells in an organ or tissue.
(D) Metaplasia, characterized by the reversible replacement of one differentiated cell type with another differentiated cell type.
(E) Neoplasia, characterized by the abnormal, uncontrolled proliferation of cells.

#### Correct Answer: D

#### High-Yield Pathophysiological Explanation:
The clinical vignette describes a patient with chronic gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), evidenced by long-standing heartburn and acid regurgitation. Endoscopy reveals salmon-pink mucosa extending proximally from the GE junction, and biopsy confirms the presence of columnar epithelium with goblet cells replacing the normal squamous epithelium of the distal esophagus. This histological finding is diagnostic of Barrett esophagus. Barrett esophagus is a classic example of metaplasia. Chronic acid exposure from GERD irritates the normal squamous epithelium of the esophagus. The basal stem cells within the squamous epithelium, under this chronic stress, undergo reprogramming. Instead of differentiating into squamous cells, they differentiate into intestinal-type columnar cells, including goblet cells, which are more resistant to acid injury. This change is metaplasia – a reversible replacement of one mature cell type (squamous) with another mature cell type (columnar with goblet cells). Barrett esophagus is a premalignant condition, increasing the risk of developing esophageal adenocarcinoma. The underlying mechanism is stem cell reprogramming driven by chronic irritation.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- (A): Dysplasia involves abnormal cell growth and differentiation, often considered a precursor to cancer. While Barrett esophagus can progress to dysplasia and then adenocarcinoma, the initial change described (replacement of squamous by columnar epithelium) is metaplasia, not dysplasia itself. Dysplasia would show loss of normal architecture and cytologic atypia.
- (B): Aplasia is the failure of an organ or tissue to develop properly, such as the failure of the lungs to develop (pulmonary aplasia). This is a developmental defect, not a response to chronic irritation in an adult.
- (C): Hyperplasia is an increase in the number of cells, leading to an enlarged organ or tissue. For example, endometrial hyperplasia involves an increase in the number of endometrial glands. While inflammation can cause hyperplasia, the key change here is a *change in cell type*, not just an increase in number.
- (E): Neoplasia refers to uncontrolled cell proliferation, forming a tumor (benign or malignant). While Barrett esophagus increases the risk of neoplasia (adenocarcinoma), the metaplastic change itself is not neoplasia. Neoplasia involves autonomous growth, whereas metaplasia is a response to stimuli and the cells are still differentiated, albeit of a different type.

#### Educational Objective (USMLE High-Yield Takeaway):
Chronic irritation (like acid reflux in GERD) can cause metaplasia, where stem cells reprogram to differentiate into a cell type better suited to withstand the stress. Barrett esophagus (squamous to columnar epithelium) is a prime example, representing a premalignant adaptation. Understand the definition and mechanism of metaplasia as a reversible change in differentiated cell type.

### Question 5: Myositis Ossificans Following Trauma
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Core Concept / 重点考点**: This question tests the understanding of myositis ossificans, a benign condition characterized by heterotopic ossification within muscle or soft tissue, typically following trauma, and the underlying mechanism of connective tissue metaplasia. (考察点：骨化性肌炎，一种在肌肉或软组织中发生的异位骨化良性疾病，通常发生在创伤后，以及潜在的结缔组织化生机制。)

#### Clinical Vignette:
A 19-year-old male collegiate soccer player sustains a forceful direct blow to his right quadriceps femoris muscle during a game. He experiences immediate pain and swelling. Six weeks later, despite conservative management, he presents with a persistent, palpable, firm, and tender mass within the affected muscle. The mass is approximately 5 cm in diameter and restricts range of motion. Radiographs of the right thigh show a well-circumscribed, spindle-shaped lesion with a dense, peripheral shell of radiopaque bone surrounding a central radiolucent area. A biopsy of the lesion reveals mature, lamellar bone trabeculae within a fibrous stroma, with minimal inflammatory infiltrate and no evidence of malignancy.

Which of the following cellular processes is the MOST likely underlying mechanism responsible for the formation of this lesion?

(A) Endochondral ossification driven by chondrocytes derived from cartilage progenitor cells.
(B) Intramembranous ossification mediated by osteoblasts differentiating directly from mesenchymal stem cells.
(C) Reactive bone formation secondary to chronic osteomyelitis involving periosteal new bone deposition.
(D) Fibrous dysplasia characterized by abnormal bone marrow elements and woven bone formation.
(E) Metastatic deposition of malignant osteoid-producing tumor cells from a primary bone sarcoma.

#### Correct Answer: B

#### High-Yield Pathophysiological Explanation:
Myositis ossificans is a benign condition characterized by heterotopic ossification (bone formation in soft tissue) typically occurring after blunt trauma to skeletal muscle. The pathogenesis involves the differentiation of local mesenchymal stem cells (pluripotent cells residing in connective tissue) into osteoblasts. This process resembles intramembranous ossification, where bone forms directly within mesenchymal connective tissue without a cartilage intermediate. The initial phase involves hematoma formation and inflammation, followed by proliferation of fibroblasts and mesenchymal cells. These mesenchymal cells then differentiate into osteoblasts, which begin depositing osteoid (unmineralized bone matrix). This osteoid subsequently mineralizes, forming mature bone. The radiographic appearance of a peripheral shell of dense bone surrounding a central radiolucent area is characteristic. The biopsy findings of mature lamellar bone within a fibrous stroma, without significant inflammation or malignant cells, confirm the benign nature and the process of direct bone formation. This contrasts with endochondral ossification (which involves a cartilage template) and other bone pathologies.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- (A): Endochondral ossification is the process by which most bones develop, involving a cartilage model that is subsequently replaced by bone. This is not the mechanism in myositis ossificans, which occurs directly within soft tissue following trauma and involves direct differentiation of mesenchymal cells into osteoblasts, bypassing the cartilage stage.
- (C): Osteomyelitis is an infection of the bone, typically leading to inflammation, bone destruction, and reactive periosteal new bone formation. While reactive bone formation occurs, the primary process is infection, and the clinical presentation (firm mass, history of trauma, lack of systemic signs of infection, biopsy findings) is inconsistent with osteomyelitis.
- (D): Fibrous dysplasia is a developmental bone disorder characterized by the replacement of normal bone marrow elements and woven bone with fibrous tissue and immature, structurally abnormal woven bone. It typically presents as monostotic or polyostotic lesions, often discovered incidentally or due to fractures, and is not typically associated with acute trauma or the specific radiographic/histologic features described.
- (E): Metastatic bone disease involves the spread of cancer cells (e.g., from prostate, breast, lung) to bone. While it can cause bone lesions, the biopsy finding of mature lamellar bone without malignant cells rules out metastasis. Furthermore, the history of trauma and the specific clinical presentation are not typical for metastatic disease.

#### Educational Objective (USMLE High-Yield Takeaway):
Myositis ossificans is a benign condition resulting from trauma-induced heterotopic ossification via direct differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells into osteoblasts (intramembranous ossification) within soft tissue. Recognize its clinical presentation, radiographic features (peripheral shell), and benign histology.

### Question 6: Acute Kidney Injury Following Hypotension
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Core Concept / 重点考点**: This question tests the understanding of the cellular mechanisms underlying reversible ischemic injury, specifically focusing on the role of ATP depletion and ion pump failure leading to hydropic swelling (细胞水肿).

#### Clinical Vignette:
A 62-year-old male with a history of hypertension and hyperlipidemia undergoes elective surgical repair of an abdominal aortic aneurysm. During the procedure, he experiences a period of prolonged hypotension (mean arterial pressure < 50 mmHg) lasting approximately 45 minutes due to significant intraoperative bleeding. Postoperatively, his urine output decreases to < 0.5 mL/kg/hr despite adequate fluid resuscitation. Laboratory tests reveal elevated serum BUN (45 mg/dL) and creatinine (3.0 mg/dL), consistent with acute kidney injury (AKI). A renal biopsy performed 24 hours post-surgery shows prominent swelling of proximal tubular epithelial cells, characterized by enlarged cytoplasm with small, clear vacuoles and loss of the distinct brush border. Electron microscopy reveals dilated mitochondria and focal disruption of microvilli.

Which of the following cellular events is the MOST likely initial mechanism responsible for the observed hydropic swelling in the renal tubular epithelial cells?

(A) Increased influx of calcium ions into the cytoplasm due to disruption of plasma membrane integrity.
(B) Activation of caspases leading to apoptotic cell death.
(C) Failure of the Na+/K+-ATPase pump due to decreased ATP production, resulting in intracellular sodium and water accumulation.
(D) Increased synthesis of ribosomal RNA leading to cytoplasmic basophilia.
(E) Formation of membrane attack complexes (MAC) by the complement system, causing cell lysis.

#### Correct Answer: C

#### High-Yield Pathophysiological Explanation:
Ischemic injury, caused by reduced blood flow and oxygen supply (hypoxia), leads to a decrease in oxidative phosphorylation within mitochondria. This results in a rapid decline in intracellular ATP levels. ATP is crucial for the function of various cellular pumps, most notably the Na+/K+-ATPase pump located in the plasma membrane. When ATP levels fall, the Na+/K+-ATPase pump fails to maintain the normal electrochemical gradient across the cell membrane (low intracellular Na+, low intracellular water). Consequently, Na+ ions accumulate inside the cell, followed by the osmotic influx of water. This leads to cellular swelling, known as hydropic swelling or vacuolar degeneration, which is a hallmark of early, reversible ischemic injury. The clinical vignette describes a scenario of prolonged hypotension leading to AKI, and the biopsy findings (swollen tubular cells, loss of brush border) are characteristic of hydropic swelling due to ischemia.

#### Distractor Analysis:
- (A): Increased influx of calcium ions is a feature of *irreversible* ischemic injury. While calcium overload can occur later due to membrane damage and mitochondrial dysfunction, it is not the *initial* mechanism causing hydropic swelling in *reversible* injury. The primary defect in reversible injury is ATP depletion.
- (B): Caspase activation is characteristic of apoptosis, a form of programmed cell death. While severe or prolonged ischemia can lead to apoptosis or necrosis, hydropic swelling is an earlier, reversible stage preceding cell death pathways like apoptosis.
- (D): Increased synthesis of ribosomal RNA and cytoplasmic basophilia are characteristic features of cellular hypertrophy or hyperplasia, often seen in response to increased functional demand, not ischemic injury. Ischemia typically leads to decreased protein synthesis due to ATP depletion.
- (E): Formation of membrane attack complexes (MAC) by the complement system typically leads to cell lysis, characteristic of necrosis or certain immune-mediated injuries. This is not the primary mechanism of reversible ischemic injury, which involves metabolic dysfunction and swelling before membrane integrity is significantly compromised.

#### Educational Objective (USMLE High-Yield Takeaway):
Reduced ATP production due to ischemia impairs the function of the Na+/K+-ATPase pump, leading to intracellular sodium and water accumulation, resulting in hydropic swelling (vacuolar degeneration), which is the earliest morphological sign of reversible cell injury.
