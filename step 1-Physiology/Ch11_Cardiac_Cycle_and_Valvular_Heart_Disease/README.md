# Chapter 11: Cardiac Cycle and Valvular Heart Disease (心动周期、心音与瓣膜性心脏病)

## 📌 Chapter Overview & Blueprint (Kaplan Medical Physiology 2023, pp. 126–137)
This chapter systematically establishes the mechanical, acoustic, and hemodynamic events of the cardiac cycle, venous pressure waveforms, heart sounds, and valvular pathophysiology. It covers:
1. **The Seven Phases of the Cardiac Cycle (Wiggers Diagram)**:
   - Phase 1: Atrial Systole (P wave, 'a wave', S4 gallop, LVEDV).
   - Phase 2: Isovolumetric Contraction (QRS, mitral closure = S1, maximal $dP/dt$, volume constant at EDV).
   - Phase 3: Rapid Ventricular Ejection (aortic valve opens, peak systolic pressure, volume plunges).
   - Phase 4: Reduced Ventricular Ejection (T wave, aortic pressure exceeds LV pressure, momentum flow, LVESV).
   - Phase 5: Isovolumetric Relaxation (aortic closure = S2, dicrotic notch, volume constant at ESV).
   - Phase 6: Rapid Ventricular Filling (mitral valve opens, ventricular volume surges, S3 gallop, 'y descent').
   - Phase 7: Reduced Ventricular Filling / Diastasis (slow filling, variable duration, truncated in tachycardia).
2. **Heart Sounds and Auscultatory Splitting**:
   - S1 ($M_1/T_1$ closure) and S2 ($A_2/P_2$ closure).
   - **Physiological Splitting**: Widens during inspiration due to delayed $P_2$ (increased RV filling).
   - **Wide Splitting**: Delayed RV ejection (Right Bundle Branch Block, Pulmonic Stenosis).
   - **Fixed Splitting**: Atrial Septal Defect (ASD; reciprocal shunt volume eliminates respiratory variation).
   - **Paradoxical (Reversed) Splitting**: Delayed LV ejection (Left Bundle Branch Block, severe Aortic Stenosis); $P_2$ precedes $A_2$; audible on expiration, fuses on inspiration.
   - **Gallops and Extra Sounds**: S3 (ventricular volume overload, early diastole), S4 (atrial contraction into stiff ventricle, late diastole; absent in AF), Opening Snap (mitral stenosis), Midsystolic Click (MVP), Ejection Click (bicuspid aortic valve).
3. **Jugular Venous Pulse (JVP) Dynamics**:
   - 'a wave' (atrial systole; giant in TS/PAH; cannon in AV dissociation/VT; absent in AF).
   - 'c wave' (isovolumetric tricuspid bulging).
   - 'x descent' (atrial relaxation and downward descent of the base; accentuated in tamponade; lost in TR).
   - 'v wave' (passive late systolic venous filling; giant 'cv wave' in TR).
   - 'y descent' (rapid early diastolic RV filling; steep dip-and-plateau in constrictive pericarditis; blunted/absent in cardiac tamponade; slow/prolonged in TS).
   - Kussmaul sign (paradoxical inspiratory JVP rise in constrictive pericarditis and RV infarction).
4. **Valvular Lesions & Hemodynamics**:
   - **Aortic Stenosis (AS)**: Massive systolic transvalvular gradient, concentric LVH, S4, pulsus parvus et tardus, Heyde syndrome.
   - **Mitral Regurgitation (MR)**: Holosystolic murmur radiating to axilla, giant PCWP 'v wave', acute (pulmonary edema) vs chronic (LA dilation).
   - **Tricuspid Regurgitation (TR)**: Holosystolic murmur, Carvallo's sign positive, giant cv waves, pulsatile liver.
   - **Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP)**: Midsystolic click and late systolic murmur; moves earlier with standing/Valsalva, later with squatting.
   - **Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM)**: Asymmetric septal hypertrophy, SAM, dynamic LVOT gradient; intensifies with Valsalva/standing, softens with squatting/handgrip; pulsus bisferiens.
   - **Aortic Regurgitation (AR)**: Early diastolic decrescendo murmur, wide pulse pressure, water-hammer (Corrigan) pulse, Quincke sign, Hill sign, Austin Flint murmur.
   - **Mitral Stenosis (MS)**: Opening snap, mid-diastolic rumble, rheumatic etiology, persistent LAP-to-LVEDP gradient, hemoptysis, Ortner syndrome.
5. **Dynamic Bedside Auscultatory Maneuvers**:
   - Respiration: Right-sided murmurs increase with inspiration (Carvallo sign); left-sided increase with expiration.
   - Valsalva Strain / Standing: Preload drops; HOCM and MVP become louder/earlier; all other murmurs soften.
   - Squatting / Passive Leg Raise: Preload and afterload rise; HOCM softens, MVP click moves later; other murmurs intensify.
   - Sustained Handgrip: Afterload rises; regurgitant murmurs (MR, AR, VSD) intensify; HOCM softens.
   - Amyl Nitrite: Afterload collapses; forward ejection murmurs (AS, HOCM) intensify; regurgitant murmurs soften.
