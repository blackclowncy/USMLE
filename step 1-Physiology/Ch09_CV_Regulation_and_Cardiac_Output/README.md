# Chapter 09: CV Regulation and Cardiac Output (心血管调节、心输出量与盖顿曲线分析)

## 📌 Chapter Overview & Blueprint (Kaplan Medical Physiology 2023, pp. 100–113)
This chapter systematically establishes the integrative neural, humoral, physical, and mechanical principles governing systemic arterial pressure and cardiac output. It covers:
1. **Short-Term Regulation of Systemic Arterial Pressure**:
   - The master hydrodynamic equation: $\text{MAP} = \text{CO} \times \text{TPR}$.
   - Arteriolar caliber as the primary site of total peripheral resistance (TPR).
   - Vasoconstrictors (Sympathetic $\alpha_1$, Angiotensin II, Vasopressin/AVP $V_1$, Endothelin-1) vs. Vasodilators (Sympathetic $\beta_2$, ANP/BNP, Nitric Oxide, Prostacyclin, Adenosine).
2. **Arterial Baroreceptor Reflex System**:
   - Anatomical sensors: Carotid sinus (Glossopharyngeal nerve, CN IX) and Aortic arch (Vagus nerve, CN X).
   - Medullary circuitry: Nucleus Tractus Solitarius (NTS) $\rightarrow$ Caudal Ventrolateral Medulla (CVLM) $\rightarrow$ Rostral Ventrolateral Medulla (RVLM) inhibition, and NTS $\rightarrow$ Nucleus Ambiguus excitation.
   - Dynamic orthostatic reflex, carotid massage, bilateral carotid occlusion, and carotid endarterectomy denervation.
3. **Guyton's Cardiovascular Equilibrium: Cardiac vs. Vascular Function Curves**:
   - Coordinate axes: Right Atrial Pressure (RAP / CVP) on the X-axis vs. Cardiac Output / Venous Return on the Y-axis.
   - Mean Systemic Filling Pressure (MSFP / $P_{sf}$): The X-intercept when cardiac output ceases ($CO = 0$); determined entirely by total intravascular blood volume and venous tone/compliance.
   - Vascular Function Curve slope: Governed by Resistance to Venous Return ($R_{VR}$) and arteriolar/venous resistance.
   - Cardiac Function Curve: The Frank-Starling performance curve shifted upward by positive inotropes (sympathetic drive, digitalis) and downward by negative inotropes (beta-blockers, heart failure, myocardial infarction).
   - Steady-State Operating Point: The unique physical intersection where $CO = VR$ and central venous pressure equilibrates.
4. **Cardiovascular Shock States & Invasive Hemodynamic Profiles**:
   - **Hypovolemic Shock**: Hemorrhage, burns, dehydration; $\text{RAP} \downarrow$, $\text{PCWP} \downarrow$, $\text{CO} \downarrow$, $\text{SVR} \uparrow$, $SvO_2 \downarrow$.
   - **Cardiogenic Shock**: Massive MI, end-stage CHF; $\text{RAP} \uparrow$, $\text{PCWP} \uparrow$, $\text{CO} \downarrow\downarrow$, $\text{SVR} \uparrow$, $SvO_2 \downarrow$.
   - **Septic / Distributive Shock**: Endotoxin vasoplegia; $\text{RAP} \downarrow/\text{nl}$, $\text{PCWP} \downarrow/\text{nl}$, $\text{CO} \uparrow$, $\text{SVR} \downarrow\downarrow$, $SvO_2 \uparrow$ (cytopathic hypoxia).
   - **Neurogenic Shock**: Cervical cord injury; loss of sympathetic tone; hypotension WITH bradycardia and warm, dry skin.
   - **Obstructive Shock**: Cardiac tamponade (diastolic equalization, pulsus paradoxus), massive PE (acute cor pulmonale, high RAP, low PCWP), and tension pneumothorax (SVC/IVC compression).
5. **High-Output Cardiac Failure**:
   - Arteriovenous fistulas (Branham's sign), severe chronic anemia (reduced blood viscosity), thyrotoxicosis ($\beta_1$ upregulation + metabolic vasodilation), wet beriberi (ATP failure vasodilation), Paget disease of bone (hypervascular shunting).
6. **Ventricular Wall Stress and Law of Laplace**:
   - Wall stress formula: $\sigma = \frac{P \cdot r}{2h}$.
   - Concentric Hypertrophy: Pressure overload (hypertension, aortic stenosis); parallel sarcomeres; thickened wall ($h \uparrow$), reduced compliance (S4, HFpEF).
   - Eccentric Hypertrophy: Volume overload (aortic/mitral regurgitation); series sarcomeres; chamber dilation ($r \uparrow$) maintaining $h/r$.
   - Determinants of $MVO_2$: Heart rate (dominant), afterload/wall tension, and contractility.
7. **Long-Term Blood Pressure Homeostasis & Endocrine Feedback**:
   - Guyton's renal-fluid feedback loop: infinite gain of pressure natriuresis and pressure diuresis; resetting in essential hypertension.
   - RAAS activation (Angiotensin II, Aldosterone), ANP/BNP guanylyl cyclase-A cGMP signaling, ADH $V_1$ vs $V_2$ pathways, Endothelin-1 $ET_A$ vs $ET_B$, Nitric Oxide eNOS signaling.
8. **Specialized Reflexes & Clinical Maneuvers**:
   - Cushing reflex (triad: hypertension, bradycardia, irregular breathing).
   - Bezold-Jarisch reflex (inferior STEMI, vagal C-fibers, hypotension-bradycardia triad).
   - Mammalian diving reflex (cold facial immersion, trigeminal afferents, bradycardia + peripheral vasoconstriction).
   - Valsalva maneuver 4-phase hemodynamic progression.
