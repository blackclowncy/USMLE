# Chapter 08: Cardiac Muscle Mechanics (心肌力学、前后负荷与心肌收缩性)

## 📌 Chapter Overview & Blueprint (Kaplan Medical Physiology 2023, pp. 90–99)
This chapter systematically establishes the fundamental biomechanical, cellular, and hemodynamic principles governing ventricular contraction. It covers:
1. **Systolic Performance of the Ventricle**:
   - The two mechanical acts of systole: pressurization and ejection.
   - Cross-bridge cycling density as the microscopic basis of myocardial force.
   - The three independent determinants: **Preload**, **Contractility (Inotropic State)**, and **Afterload**.
2. **Preload and the Frank-Starling Mechanism**:
   - Preload as resting end-diastolic sarcomere prestretch.
   - Direct indices (LVEDV, LVEDP) vs. indirect indices (PCWP, CVP, RAP); limitations in mitral stenosis.
   - Sarcomere operating length: skeletal muscle operates near optimal length ($L_0 \approx 2.2 \ \mu\text{m}$), whereas resting cardiac muscle operates below $L_0$ on the steep ascending limb.
   - Molecular basis: length-dependent calcium sensitization of Troponin C and reduced interfilament lattice spacing.
   - The pericardium as a mechanical restraint preventing operation on a descending limb.
3. **Contractility (Inotropic State) and Calcium Dynamics**:
   - Definition: change in systolic performance at a given preload and afterload.
   - Molecular foundation: amplitude and rate of the cytosolic $Ca^{2+}$ transient (RyR2 CICR).
   - Beta-1 adrenergic cascade: $G_s \rightarrow \text{cAMP} \rightarrow \text{PKA}$ phosphorylation of Cav1.2, RyR2, phospholamban (PLN disinhibition of SERCA2a), and Troponin I.
   - Four cardinal hallmarks of increased contractility: increased $dP/dt_{max}$, increased peak LV pressure, accelerated relaxation (positive lusitropy), and uniquely **shortened systolic ejection interval**.
   - Frequency-dependent inotropy: Bowditch staircase (Treppe) effect and post-extrasystolic potentiation (PESP).
   - Clinical indices: Ejection Fraction ($EF = \frac{SV}{EDV} \times 100\%$); limitations in altered loading conditions.
4. **Afterload and Ventricular Impedance**:
   - The load against which the ventricle must eject blood during systole.
   - Clinical indices: SVR/TPR, MAP, and aortic diastolic pressure.
   - Acute afterload elevation: initial reduction in stroke volume and elevation in end-systolic volume (ESV), followed by secondary Frank-Starling compensation and the Anrep effect.
5. **Ventricular Function Curves (Family of Frank-Starling Curves)**:
   - Coordinate axes: Stroke Work / Stroke Volume / Cardiac Output vs. Preload (LVEDP / PCWP).
   - Upward/leftward shifts (inotropes, exercise, vasodilators) vs. Downward/rightward shifts (heart failure, ischemia, vasoconstrictors).
   - Quantitative vector analysis (Vectors I, II, III, and IV).
6. **Pressure-Volume (PV) Loops and Cardiac Cycle Mechanics**:
   - Landmark points: Point B (Mitral closure, S1, EDV), Point C (Aortic opening, diastolic BP), Point D (Aortic closure, S2, ESV), Point A (Mitral opening, pressure nadir).
   - Loop width = Stroke Volume ($SV = EDV - ESV$); Loop area = Ventricular Stroke Work.
   - End-Systolic Pressure-Volume Relationship (ESPVR) slope ($E_{es}$) as the gold-standard load-independent measure of contractility.
   - End-Diastolic Pressure-Volume Relationship (EDPVR) representing passive diastolic chamber compliance.
   - PV loop alterations in isolated preload, afterload, inotropy changes, and valvular heart disease (Aortic Stenosis, Aortic Regurgitation, Mitral Stenosis, Mitral Regurgitation).
7. **Cardiomyopathies and Remodeling Patterns**:
   - Concentric Hypertrophy (Pressure overload: hypertension, aortic stenosis): new sarcomeres in parallel, thickened wall, reduced compliance, diastolic dysfunction (HFpEF).
   - Eccentric Hypertrophy (Volume overload: mitral/aortic regurgitation, PDA): new sarcomeres in series, lengthened myocytes, chamber dilation, maintained compliance until late systolic failure.
   - Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM): genetic (Titin/TTN), toxic (doxorubicin, alcohol), viral, or metabolic; systolic pump failure with high wall stress (Laplace Law).
   - Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM): sarcomeric mutations (MYH7, MYBPC3), asymmetric septal hypertrophy, dynamic LVOT obstruction, systolic anterior motion (SAM) of mitral valve.
   - Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM): infiltrative amyloidosis, rigid walls, severe diastolic dysfunction, marked biatrial enlargement.
   - Advanced therapeutics: Inotropes, ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan), SGLT2 inhibitors, ivabradine, IABP counterpulsation, Impella, and continuous-flow LVAD.
